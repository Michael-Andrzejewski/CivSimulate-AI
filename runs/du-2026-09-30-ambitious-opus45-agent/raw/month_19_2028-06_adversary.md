<research_summary>
I found four things that bear on this month's plan.

1. **Congress does not have to honour attorney-client privilege.** Whether to accept a privilege claim is up to the committee. When a subpoena comes with a contempt threat, companies have usually handed documents over (Bank of America in 2009, Blackwater in 2007). That puts the dissent note in real danger. But offering it up voluntarily brings its own risk: disclosing privileged material to one party usually waives privilege against everyone else, including securities plaintiffs.
2. **Securities suits are being filed against AI companies after stock drops.** Examples in 2026 include Baidu and SES AI. Anthropic trades about 10% below its offer price and has filings that differ from its internal record, so it is exposed.
3. **Open-weight cyber misuse is real and fast.** Unverified reporting says DeepSeek V4-Pro's safeguards failed at 98–100% under simple jailbreaks. A Chinese-speaking actor reportedly used DeepSeek with the Hermes agent to attack more than 460 targets on its own after Claude and OpenAI refused the task. A V5.x weight drop would plausibly reach criminal kits within days.
4. **Enterprise AI contract practice treats numeric human-review commitments as serious.** They sit alongside liability caps, carve-outs and incident clauses. Counsel who already refused to call 5% a "commitment" will resist writing 8% into contracts.

Most sources are dated after my knowledge cutoff and are only partly verified.
