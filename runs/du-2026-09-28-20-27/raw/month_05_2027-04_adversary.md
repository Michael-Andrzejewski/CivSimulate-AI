<research_summary>
Real-world evidence points in a few directions. First, measuring eval-awareness is still unsettled science. Apollo has concluded that evaluations "could not offer reliable evidence of actual alignment." Several 2026 papers disagree on whether awareness changes behaviour at all. So a larger May sample could still produce a contested or ambiguous result, and training directly against detection cues invites the charge that the model is being taught to hide. Second, the FDA's operative guidance (Feb 3, 2026) puts patch development and deployment inside the manufacturer's own quality-management and CAPA processes. That gives device vendors a lawful basis to refuse or slow-walk third-party-engineered patches, and CDRH has little reason to issue a fast new clarification. Third, the Remote Access Security Act passed the House 369–22, so Senate hawks, not open-source advocates, are the pivotal audience. A BIS comment that carves out open weights could annoy the hawks who have been quoting Anthropic while still failing to win over Delangue and a16z. Fourth, rural-hospital ransomware has a high base rate: about a third of rural attacks cause ambulance diversion, and 2026 already had two major incidents. A copycat in April, possibly at a Glasswing intake organisation, is plausible. Finally, UK AISI (£66m budget, 100+ technical staff) has used METR for independent reviews. That raises capacity and conflict-of-interest questions if METR becomes Anthropic's reverifier, a standard-setter and a potential IPO auditor all at once.
