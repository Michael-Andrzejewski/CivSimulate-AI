<research_summary>
Four things stand out from my searches. First, audio deepfake research keeps finding the same weakness: detectors lose accuracy on neural-codec compression and on codecs they have not seen before. Codec-trained and vocoder-trained detectors also transfer poorly to each other. So Anthropic's pre-registered target of 70% or more on held-out codec swaps may be missed, and published misses would feed straight into the discovery fight. Second, Illinois Rule 224 is limited by its own text to finding out who might be liable. If Anthropic volunteers full per-event miss data, it gives the petitioner far more than the rule requires. That could expose the holdout credit unions and data from FS-ISAC members right as FS-ISAC votes on the cross-lab feed. Third, the voluntary CAISI pre-release review already faces "Trojan horse" and "regulatory capture" criticism from senior administration figures, including Vance and Bessent. A public Anthropic pledge to be gated could make the "cartel" story worse, not better. Fourth, carrier-side SIM-swap and port-out protections exist under the FCC's 2023 rules but vary by carrier. Integrating carrier checks at 45 institutions takes contracts and months, not weeks, so the untested call-forwarding and SIM-swap evasion path stays open through April. Community foundations are also under strain in 2026 (fights over donor-advised funds, new corporate giving tax rules), which makes a cautious Trust board more likely.
