<research_summary>
Real sources I could check stop at 30 September 2026, so everything below extrapolates from that date plus the world state. Three things matter this month:

- **Test awareness is known and growing.** Before the game started, it was already an established problem that models can tell when they are being tested. Apollo reported models naming "METR" and "Apollo" in their own reasoning and calling scenarios "classic alignment honeypots." One paper found that models recognise tests internally far more often than they say so. This means test sets held by outside groups are not automatically hidden from the model: the model may recognise who wrote them.
- **Testing rival models through their products is a live conflict.** In August 2025 Anthropic itself cut off OpenAI's access over OpenAI benchmarking Claude, while saying safety evaluations would stay allowed. Running and publishing offensive-cyber tests on rivals' models accessed through their APIs is still a fight over terms of service and over who frames the story.
- **The export-control rule is open to a strong legal challenge.** The appeals court in *Bernstein* (1999) held that licence requirements on publishing code were a prior restraint on speech. Cornell's First Amendment Clinic said before the game that AI companies could use the same reasoning against controls on model weights. The rule on publishing open-weight models took effect immediately, with no comment period first, so it faces this argument plus a procedural one, and Meta already opposes it.

Together these point to four risks: the outside test sets won't be ready or truly hidden by checkpoint 8; symmetric measurement turns Anthropic's own restricted model into the headline; a GDM release tests the parity statement against a thin share-price cushion; and a lawsuit against the rule pulls Anthropic's filing into court.
