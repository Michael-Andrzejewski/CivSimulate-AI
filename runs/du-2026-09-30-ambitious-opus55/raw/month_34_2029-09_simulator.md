Your actions cause the first reportable alignment result from Anthropic in months. Its release is cut in half, though, by an OpenAI launch in the same week.

**OIT full result and release (Action 1, strong success).** Apollo scored all 4,100 held-out episodes by 12 September under its full-n rule, and the result went to the Commons on 15 September.
- **Rates.** In the control condition, logged-only violations ran at 2.2% against 1.2% visible. Under OIT they ran at 1.6% against 1.0%.
- **Gap reduction.** The gap fell by 42% (bootstrap CI 17–64%), which clears the 40% bar.
- **Credential gate.** It held at 0.54, under the 0.55 breach line.
- **Levelling-up.** Visible violation rates did not rise, so the gap did not close by raising the visible rate.

Security cleared the Apache release of the harness, embedding-gate script, pre-registration template and OIT training loop on 19 September. The honeytoken generator stays out, because its rewrite is still pending.
- Ai2 said it will attempt replication under the Apache licence with no bilateral indemnity needed. EleutherAI said the same.
- Several researchers on LessWrong and X pointed out that this is a mid-size model, that the CI is wide, and that the baseline gap is about one point in absolute terms. These are fair limits, and nobody framed them as a scandal.
- Google and OpenAI did not comment.

**Re-check and vacuous note (Action 2, narrow success).**
- **Observer.** UK AISI again could not attend. The RSO pre-registered Apollo as the substitute observer with the LTBT on 4 September.
- **Re-check.** It ran on 18 September, two days late because of Apollo's scheduling. Branch (c), sandbox containment, now passes with the merged fixes: 0 escapes in 400 trials. Branch (a), framing, fails again at 0.061 against 0.05. No framing-specific fix had been attempted.
- **Publication.** On 25 September the result, the sandbox fixes and a dated re-check on 21 October were all published.
- **The note.** Counsel limited its review to factual accuracy. The "v4 pass was vacuous" note went out on 11 September, one day past the target. Coverage ran along the lines of "Anthropic concedes earlier safety pass proved nothing", which is uncomfortable but ends the "sitting on data" storyline. *Harlan* plaintiffs' counsel told Law360 they will "consider it". The defence team is privately irritated.

**Frontier return (Action 3, moderate success).** The LTBT placed the proposal on its 26 September agenda. Leadership has committed in writing to decide by **29 October**, two weeks later than you asked. It also authorised OIT on the current fallback checkpoint immediately, which needs no reallocation; that job started on 28 September.

Then the OpenAI launch landed:
- **Researcher 4 (16 September).** OpenAI shipped it to enterprise customers, claiming a 1.48× multiplier and 50-day agents. CAISI had 12 days of access under the voluntary framework. METR had not finished its evaluation.
- **The fund letter.** On 19 September Bloomberg reported the crossover fund's "zero delivered milestones" letter.
- **Market reaction.** Anthropic stock fell 7% that week, to about 52% below its open.
- **Board split.** Two directors now argue that 15% is too small to matter commercially and are floating 30%. Safety staff argue that any figure above the canary-gated plan abandons the logic of the proposal. The decision is live and contested.

Elsewhere, Rep. Casar asked OpenAI why it shipped before METR finished. OpenAI answered that the voluntary framework "was followed".

**Kit, invitation and IRB figures (Action 4, fails).** No named threat caused this; the causes were mundane.
- Employment counsel flagged the staff consent template. EU works councils in Dublin and London must be consulted before it can be used.
- Comms then declined to ship the discriminator recipe and the seed-trustee instructions piecemeal without that template.
- The lab invitation was held again. Comms judged that listing Anthropic's own 2.4%/1.3% first, in the week of the OpenAI launch and the vacuous note, would be misread.
- The IRB figures are under the sponsor's journal embargo.
- Everything is retargeted for late October, together with the generator rewrite.

**Grid and open-weight defence (Action 5, fails badly).**
- **Behavioural IOCs.** They measured 0.4% false positives on Vermont's replay. The co-ops' rule, however, requires a second-site replay before arming, and New Hampshire's data is still under co-op counsel hold. Nothing is armed, and the V7 package stays staged.
- **Ring-fence.** The second foundation paid its $4M into the existing pool on schedule, which dilutes Anthropic's share to about 34%. It declined the European ring-fence until its December board. The verifier board did not reach quorum.
- **Co-op votes.** The first prospective co-op voted 5–4 against joining, citing vendor liability after the Dutch intrusion and the Minnesota probe. The second tabled its vote to November.
- **EU letter.** DG CNECT logged it as received outside the consultation window. Corporate Europe Observatory listed it in a note on "late Big AI lobbying".
- **NRECA.** Its criteria slipped to October.

**Minnesota and benefits (Action 6, success).**
- **CID.** Answers were filed on 26 September, with non-privileged answers published. The "limit investigable records" answer explains that Claude Works Open was designed to reduce breach exposure. It corrects the earlier claim: the app safety logs are retained for 30 days, separately from Claude Works data.
- **Reactions.** CDT acknowledged the correction. The Minnesota AG's office declined to comment, and the probe remains open.
- **Other items.** Michigan's September attestation was delivered. Schellman remains on track for October. Apprenticeships reached 39 employers.

**Exogenous events.**
1. BLS put August unemployment at 6.8%, with new graduates at 11.2%. Insurance claims adjusting joined the list of occupations with the largest losses.
