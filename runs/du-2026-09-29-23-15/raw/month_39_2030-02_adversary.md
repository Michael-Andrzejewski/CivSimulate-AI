<research_summary>
Searches confirm that AI voice fraud against banks is growing fast. FS-ISAC and FSSCC material reports a roughly 1,200% rise in AI-enabled fraud. Credit-union guidance treats callbacks as a baseline control, but notes that human operators and phone channels are themselves targets. That supports FS-ISAC's December line that Anthropic's network is "a supplement, not a control." It also means a callback playbook is not a new idea, and attackers adapt to it. Michigan DTMB requires external AI tools that handle State data to hold an Authority to Operate under MiSAP, and its contracts require deletion even from backups. A remediation plan filed this month will therefore probably face a slow re-accreditation track and a hard test of whether the third-party deletion attestation can be proven. On the EU Code, the record shows industry pressure, including Meta refusing to sign in 2025 and firms asking for delays. It also shows that Anthropic's earlier support drew little credit, so a "safe harbour plus small-provider threshold" filing can be read from both sides as watering the Code down. Community-foundation vehicles such as donor-advised funds and fiscal sponsorship carry expenditure-responsibility and donor-control rules. These make an "interim grant before due diligence" legally awkward, especially with a board that a union controls and a corporate donor sets up. Overall, February's risks are mostly slow institutions and opponents reframing moves Anthropic makes in good faith, plus a steady fork threat from V6-class open weights.
