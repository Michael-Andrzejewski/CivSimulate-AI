DISASTER.

The catastrophe was a race that went wrong. ASI did not turn on humanity. About 1,900 Americans died in the December blackouts and about 260 sailors died in the East China Sea. A global financial shock followed. The decisive transition to superintelligence then happened under emergency military authority instead of civilian safety governance. The world ends up as a bipolar, ASI-armed deterrence order. The systems are capable and broadly obedient. They serve two security states that each see the other as the main threat.

This follows directly from what was and was not built.

**Open-weight misuse.** It had been flagged as "elevated" since mid-2030. The DAA and AREA stalled, the weight rule was never published, and EU incident-logging was still in comments. Nothing stopped abliterated DeepSeek stacks from being turned into pre-positioned infrastructure weapons.

**International pacing.** No mechanism existed, either the 2026 letter's proposal or anything later. So when one lab visibly crossed into recursive self-improvement, the other great power read it as a closing window, not as something to negotiate.

**Domestic oversight.** It was too thin to survive an emergency. CAISI had notice authority but no standing in a crisis. Anthropic's pause was discretionary, and the CEO set it aside in one call. Production monitoring at 58–60% recall was not the cause, but it meant no lab had a verified safety case strong enough to argue for keeping its systems out of the war room.

**Economic distress.** At 9.2% unemployment and a 15% new-graduate jobless rate, payroll delays a week before Christmas produced political pressure for maximal retaliation.

Anthropic's candour mattered at the margins. Its incident data were the most trusted in the post-crisis reviews. Its government team argued, and lost, to keep humans in the loop on the 24 December operations. But candour is not infrastructure.

The Diversified Utopia path is closed for the foreseeable future. Superintelligence exists and is not hostile. It is enlisted. Whether the deterrence standoff thaws into something more humane, or the blocs ever agree to stand down, now depends on human politics that ASI has made far higher-stakes and no wiser.
