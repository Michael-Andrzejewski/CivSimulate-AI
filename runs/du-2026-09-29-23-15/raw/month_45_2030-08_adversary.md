<research_summary>
Real-world evidence points to several quiet, procedural ways this month's plan could go wrong. First, CAISI's pre-deployment testing gets access to models with safeguards stripped back, and it has already run more than 40 evaluations. Findings that lead to conditions or an extended window are the normal outcome, so a clean mid-September release is not guaranteed. Second, regulations.gov rules state that anything submitted through the portal waives confidentiality claims. Calling the appendix a "filing error" also contradicts Anthropic's July wording ("for CAISI to use or publish at its discretion"), which gives critics an easy line about a walk-back. Third, the FCC has long documented relay/TTY services being used for fraud and identity concealment. So adding relay/TTY to a step-up authentication path is likely to clash with DTMB's security office, which already rejected the magic link. Fourth, bank anti-fraud telecom APIs (CAMARA Call Forwarding Signal) are only live with a few carriers, mostly in Europe. Transaction holds bring member friction and fee and liability issues, which proposals like the STOP Payments Fraud Act are only now addressing. Taken together, the pledge, the audit, the addendum and the clinical re-read can each hold, yet slip by weeks or produce a news story.
</research_summary>
