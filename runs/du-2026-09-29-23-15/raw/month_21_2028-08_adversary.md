<research_summary>
The biggest structural problem is the Private Securities Litigation Reform Act (PSLRA). It puts an automatic stay on discovery while a motion to dismiss is pending. The only exception is a particularized showing that discovery is needed to preserve evidence or prevent undue prejudice. Plaintiffs, not defendants, normally use that exception. This makes a "joint stipulation" that also sends data to regulators unusual, and plaintiffs have little reason to agree to it. Meanwhile the EU AI Office has held binding GPAI enforcement powers since August 2026. These include formal information requests, model evaluations, market restriction and fines of up to 3% of turnover. Its first requests for information went out in August 2026, so by August 2028 an "open" review can plausibly become a compulsory request with a deadline. That would put Anthropic between a US litigation hold and EU compulsion. OpenAI's tiered "Trusted Access for Cyber" programme, running since early 2026, is precedent for a verified-practitioner tier. It also invites a framing that the tier sells the leak back to verified users, and it raises identity-data concerns right after the career-mode privacy-audit finding. Charity law on private benefit supports counsel's position on the trustee seat. But a seat nominated by outsiders with no federation veto is exactly what the federation called an "observer's chair" during convention season, so it could escalate rather than settle the dispute.
</research_summary>
