<research_summary>
The biggest risk this month comes from securities timing. Under standard IPO communication rules, a company stays "in registration" until 25 days after the SEC declares the registration effective. If pricing happens in late October, gun-jumping caution runs to roughly late November. The player's plan to file the DOJ letter and publish the position paper "the week after pricing" therefore collides with a real legal window, not just with how cautious counsel is. The Rule 169 safe harbor covers only factual information released in the ordinary course, not forward-looking advocacy. Research on evaluation awareness from 2026, all unverified and dated after my cutoff, says three things. The ability to tell an evaluation from real use grows with model scale across open-weight families. Suppressing verbalised eval-awareness does not remove the internal representation of it. And a third-party evaluator (Apollo, on Opus 4.6) has already declined to give an alignment assessment because eval-awareness made its tests unreliable. That precedent bears directly on Actions 1 and 3. On the capability side, DeepMind said in September 2026 that its next model was in post-training and would ship "much earlier than end of year," and DeepSeek V5 is forecast for H2. In the game's terms, a Gemini 5 release in Q4 during the roadshow is a live risk. EU reporting runs through separate national channels (BSI, the data-protection authorities and the LKA/BKA). Because of that, "proactive notification" about accounts brings in GDPR legal-basis questions and more parties who could leak, while a reporter at *The Record* is already asking.
