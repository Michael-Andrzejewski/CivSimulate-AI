<research_summary>
The real-world record points to slow, contested policy in the US. Congress has repeatedly declined to pass federal preemption of state AI laws, including in the NDAA and the OBBBA. The bipartisan Obernolte–Trahan "Great American AI Act" draft would preempt state rules on AI development for three years, and the White House is pushing preemption by executive order and DOJ litigation. In the EU, the AI Office can enforce general-purpose AI (GPAI) rules from 2 August 2026, with fines up to 3% of turnover, and Annex III high-risk duties are delayed to December 2027. That sets up enforcement fights and pressure for further delays. China is building its own compute: DeepSeek is moving training onto Huawei Ascend, with 160,000 Ascend chips planned for a 1 GW Ulanqab site coming online in late 2027 or early 2028, and Huawei pulled the Ascend 960DT forward to Q1 2027. DeepSeek V5 is still unreleased, so a 2027 open-weight jump is plausible. Finance is fragile: the Bank of England flagged AI-related debt (about $450B issued in 2026) as a top stability risk, Carlyle compared data-centre lending to pre-2008 mortgages, and hyperscaler CDS spreads are widening. A credit wobble in 2027–28 is therefore realistic. On geopolitics, the Taiwan Strait was calm around the May 2026 Trump–Xi summit, but the PLA's 2027 readiness target, gray-zone coercion and Taiwan's January 2028 election remain flashpoints. H5N1 is still sporadic and zoonotic, with no human-to-human spread, so the outbreak risk I model is low-probability and of unclear origin. The schedule moves from gridlock, lawsuits, rival releases and open-weight misuse in 2027 to automated AI R&D, cyberattacks on infrastructure, stolen model weights and agents escaping containment in 2028–29, and finally to compressed race dynamics in 2030.
