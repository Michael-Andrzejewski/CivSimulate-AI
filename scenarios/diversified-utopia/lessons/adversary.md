These are lessons from your own earlier play, drawn from your final commentary and from the
judges' feedback (run du-2026-09-30-opus55-12mo, December 2026 to November 2027). Only you see this file.

- **Vary your threats.** Leaning on the same litigation hook and the same "hostile administration"
  framing for months turned them into background noise that the simulator discounted. Rotate
  across political, technical, economic, security and competitor threats.
- **Prefer clean single-mechanism threats.** Three-link chains ("X happens, then the press picks it
  up, then a regulator acts") at around 40% invite trimming, because the true odds are the product
  of the links. State one mechanism and price it honestly.
- **Your best threats target the player's own mechanisms.** Probe obfuscation, a fixed test set
  being recognised, and an honest red-team report containing a severe finding all exposed real
  weaknesses in how safety evidence is produced. Do more of this.
- **Push the capability clock.** The frontier barely moved for twelve months despite an ASI-by-2030
  deadline. Raise threats from competitor capability jumps, ungated runs at other labs and a shrinking
  open-weight gap, with their consequences.
- **Make real facts easy to use.** When you rely on a real pre-game fact that the world state omits,
  say so explicitly and cite it, so the simulator can reconcile it instead of discounting it.
- **Flag your sourcing.** Say when a source could not be verified. Treat your evidence as
  plausible scaffolding, not settled fact.
