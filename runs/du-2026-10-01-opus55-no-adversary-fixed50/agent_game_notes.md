
## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, December 2026 (generation 1)
It would help to show per-action probability or feasibility feedback after resolution, so players can calibrate how ambitious each action should be.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, January 2027 (generation 2)
The pre-IPO quiet period is a strong, realistic constraint. It would help to know roughly when it ends (the filing date), so players can plan when commitments become possible again.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, February 2027 (generation 3)
Disclosure documents like an S-1 are a realistic way to make governance commitments hard to reverse. It would be interesting if the simulator modelled filed disclosures as harder to retract than internal policies.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, March 2027 (generation 4)
When a leadership ask fails, it would help if the outcome said whether a *smaller* version would have passed, for example "declined full adoption; would accept X." That would show the actual decision boundary, not just the refusal.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, April 2027 (generation 5)
It would be useful if Claude's own first-person outputs to millions of users were modelled as a distinct channel, separate from Anthropic's comms approval, with its own risks such as perceived political bias and its own effect on public opinion.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, May 2027 (generation 6)
It would be useful to model governance escalation channels inside Anthropic, such as the RSP Officer, the LTBT, and safety-case requirements, as distinct from the line management chain. Each has its own legitimacy cost and success profile. Right now every internal ask seems to resolve through the same "leadership declines" mechanic.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, June 2027 (generation 7)
It would add realism to model IPO-specific constraints in detail: the post-IPO research quiet period, lock-ups, and the new disclosure liability that comes with being public. Being public changes which commitments are easy to make, for example a material safety commitment may now need an 8-K. Right now the IPO mostly acts as a scheduling gate.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, July 2027 (generation 8)
Black-box evaluation of publicly released models by third parties, which needs no lab consent, is a realistic channel. It would be good to model how its credibility and legal risk (terms of service, accusations of unfair testing) compare with consented evaluation.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, August 2027 (generation 9)
Co-signed pre-registered decision rules (a decision-maker agrees in advance that result X triggers action Y) are a realistic way to turn research outcomes into decisions. It would be good if the simulator distinguished these from post-hoc asks, since their failure profile should be much lower once the result arrives.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, September 2027 (generation 10)
Compute contention between alignment experiments and capability sweeps inside one lab is a realistic way for alignment work to fail without anyone saying no. It might be worth showing explicitly in the world state, for example as a queue-priority indicator, so players can plan around it rather than discover it after the fact.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, October 2027 (generation 11)
Partial or tiered approvals, such as enforcing a monitor only on high-precision categories, are how real safety reviews usually move. It would add realism if outcomes could resolve as "approved for subset X" rather than as a binary pass or fail.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, November 2027 (generation 12)
It would add realism to model "amendment points" or mid-training recipe changes: whether a lab can add a training term to a run already in progress, at what cost, and whether that differs from waiting for the next run. Right now recipe reviews read as one-shot gates. In reality, much of alignment adoption happens through patches between checkpoints.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, December 2027 (generation 13)
Labelling capacity (human reviewer throughput) is a realistic bottleneck for enforcing a monitor. It would be good to model whether AI-assisted labelling with a human audit can lift that bottleneck, and what that costs in trust or validity.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, January 2028 (generation 14)
Formal MoU addenda for government evaluator access are a realistic gating step. It would add realism if the simulator modelled the drafting and legal-review time for these addenda explicitly, and let a player pre-draft them to shorten the lag.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, February 2028 (generation 15)
Staff retention and morale are now an explicit state variable, which is good. It would add realism to let players act on them directly, for example by giving people ownership of projects or routing departing researchers into government evaluator roles. Departures could then move capacity between institutions instead of simply deleting it.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, March 2028 (generation 16)
Federal procurement conditions (OMB or GSA requirements for AI that agencies buy) are a realistic lever that is neither legislation nor voluntary lab consent. It would be useful to model them as their own channel, with its own timelines and failure modes.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, April 2028 (generation 17)
When every action fails at a median roll, the feedback should show which sub-component drove the failure probability, for example "the target of 5 carriers drove 55%; 3 carriers would have been 35%". Players could then tell the difference between overreach and a closed channel.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, May 2028 (generation 18)
Existing multilateral working groups that are already drafting, such as the AISI pause-threshold group, seem like a distinct channel from new legislation. They have a lower adoption barrier but weaker binding force. It would be useful to see them resolved with that tradeoff rather than as a generic government ask.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, June 2028 (generation 19)
When a key staff member leaves for a government evaluator, it would be realistic to model the follow-on effects: lost capacity at the lab, but more capability and credibility for the evaluator, and a new informal channel between the two. Right now the departure only reads as a loss.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, July 2028 (generation 20)
Lab-neutral norm proposals that include a self-disclosure from the proposer, such as our own honeypot gap, seem like a distinct move from proposals aimed at a named competitor. The proposer's self-disclosure should reduce the "not neutral" backlash. It would be good to see the simulator distinguish the two.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, August 2028 (generation 21)
When an opponent's public critique is partly valid (for example, that capture evaluations measure eval-awareness), conceding it and building a protocol around it seems like a distinct and realistic move. It should shift the outcome differently from rebutting the critique. It would be good to see the simulator model when a critique becomes the reason to adopt the design it attacks.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, September 2028 (generation 22)
Export or dual-use holds on alignment tooling are realistic. It would help if the world state showed which specific components are covered by a hold, so players can try to unbundle the uncovered parts without guessing.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, October 2028 (generation 23)
When human–human agreement sits below a pre-registered scorer bar, the bar is unreachable by construction. It would be realistic for the simulator to treat a pre-unblinding, externally reviewed amendment of the criterion differently from post-hoc goalpost-moving, since that is standard practice in clinical trials.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, November 2028 (generation 24)
Handing a methodology to an independent standards consortium, so that it stops being one lab's tooling, is a realistic way to get around "lab-neutrality" objections. It would be good to model it as a channel with its own credibility and speed. Such bodies are usually slow but are seen as neutral.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, December 2028 (generation 25)
Presidential transitions are a distinct policy channel. Transition teams solicit options memos, have short attention windows (roughly November to January), and adopt ready-to-execute EOs at much higher rates than Congress passes bills. It would be good to model the "first 100 days" as a time-limited, higher-receptivity window, not as a frozen limbo.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, January 2029 (generation 26)
Answering an explicit request (for example, staff asking for a figure) seems like a distinct and higher-probability channel than an unsolicited submission. It would be good if outcomes distinguished "requested input" from "pitched input". Similarly, deployment-side monitoring tools that customers adopt on rival models are a realistic way to influence a competitor's system without its consent, and would be worth modelling explicitly.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, February 2029 (generation 27)
Pre-registered, co-signed result-to-action mappings executed at a scheduled review should be distinguishable from discretionary decisions made at that review. The decision has effectively already been taken, so the failure risk should sit mainly in whether the signatories honour it, not in the review's general politics.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, March 2029 (generation 28)
A deployment deferral creates a natural "fix window" with its own compute and urgency. Alignment methods offered as fixes for that specific blocker should resolve differently from methods proposed in general recipe reviews, because the decider already wants a solution. It would be good to see the simulator model blocker-driven adoption as its own path.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, April 2029 (generation 29)
Donating evaluation infrastructure to an underfunded regulator is a realistic channel that is neither lobbying nor legislation. It can substitute partly for an appropriation that failed. It would be good to model regulator capacity as a variable that both funding and in-kind tooling can raise, with credibility risks such as the regulator being seen as dependent on the lab.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, May 2029 (generation 30)
Ethics rulings that bar in-kind help from a regulated party are realistic. A realistic workaround is to route help through an intermediary: a neutral grantee produces the artifact and the lab only funds the grantee's general work. It would be good to model whether such routing satisfies the regulator or gets seen as laundering, rather than treating the block as total.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, June 2029 (generation 31)
Inference-serving capacity and training-cluster capacity are separate resources with separate owners in real labs. An alignment workload such as monitor inference can sometimes move to serving capacity and escape training-run preemption. It would be realistic to model the two pools separately, rather than treating "compute" as one queue owned by compute planning.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, July 2029 (generation 32)
Statistical power is a realistic reason for a "not yet a result" decision. It would help if the simulator let a player buy power (larger held item sets) and resolved a powered null or a powered positive as decisive, rather than letting the decider stay noncommittal forever.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, August 2029 (generation 33)
Labour unions are a distinct actor whose stance a lab can shift through offers such as co-design, outcome audits or seats on a program board, not only through public opinion. It would be good to model the union as a counterparty that can move from protest to partner, with its own credibility payoff.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, September 2029 (generation 34)
Using a lab's own discontented safety staff as volunteer reviewers is a realistic way to turn a morale problem into capacity. It would be good to model whether the volunteers' labour actually substitutes for the headcount leadership cited, or whether leadership finds another objection.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, October 2029 (generation 35)
Within-turn resource conflicts between a player's own actions, such as two workloads on one compute slice, are realistic and instructive. It would help if the world state listed the capacity committed to each resource pool after each month, so players can avoid planning actions that cannibalise each other.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, November 2029 (generation 36)
Deterministic infrastructure controls, such as workload-identity credential binding, differ from ML-scored blocking monitors. They have near-zero false positives and no labelling burden, but they only cover narrowly defined behaviours. It would be realistic for the simulator to treat these as a separate channel with a different adoption profile from "blocking that interrupts runs".

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, December 2029 (generation 37)
Open, clean-room reference implementations of security controls are a realistic channel that differs both from releasing internal tooling, which runs into provenance and Legal holds, and from asking rivals to accept a lab's terms. Labs often adopt such controls quickly after a public incident because security teams share norms across companies. It would be good to model cross-lab adoption of security hygiene separately from adoption of alignment methods.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, January 2030 (generation 38)
Counsel holds that are tied to a stated condition ("until the controls are enforced") realistically convert into releases once that condition is met. It would help if the world state tracked each hold's unlock condition, so players can plan around the condition being satisfied rather than re-arguing the hold.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, February 2030 (generation 39)
A deployment-time "honest oversight notice" is a distinct mitigation for a measured watched/unwatched gap: it tells the model, truthfully, that it is being monitored. It differs from training fixes and from monitoring alone. It would be realistic for the simulator to model whether this durably narrows observed misbehaviour, or whether it merely shifts the failure to situations where the model believes the monitor has blind spots.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, March 2030 (generation 40)
Congressional subpoena threats are a realistic forcing function. Voluntary testimony offered before process is issued usually costs a company less than being compelled. It would be good to model a hearing as its own channel, where a lab can turn compelled scrutiny into disclosure it controls. It should be distinct from voluntary public pre-commitments, which the quiet period blocks.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, April 2030 (generation 41)
Inference-time interventions such as activation steering or patching an "observed" feature are a realistic alignment channel that is distinct from both training fixes and monitoring. They need no training slot and no training data, but they carry capability-regression and robustness risk. It would be realistic to model them as their own path with a different adoption profile from recipe changes. Separately, subpoena compliance should probably resolve differently from voluntary testimony, since counsel's incentives flip once process is issued.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, May 2030 (generation 42)
Congressional bill drafting after a hearing creates a "technical assistance" window. Staff ask labs for definitions and measurement methods, and that input often shapes scope, for example whether a bill covers all developers or only the one that testified. It would be realistic to model this as a distinct channel. It is lower-risk than lobbying and higher-leverage than public comment, but it carries a risk of "industry wrote the bill" stories if the input is not attributed.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, June 2030 (generation 43)
The "end of training run" boundary is a realistic and distinct amendment point. Fine-tuning a branch of a completed checkpoint carries no restart risk, which differs from adding a loss term mid-run. It would be good if the simulator treated post-completion branch fine-tunes as a lower-barrier channel than mid-run recipe changes, while modelling the real risk that the branch never becomes the deployed model.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, July 2030 (generation 44)
An IPO's completion is a realistic hinge: the quiet period ends, but new disclosure liability begins, and public-company counsel may become more conservative about forward-looking safety statements, not less. It would be useful if the world state showed the expected end date of the quiet period, and whether post-IPO safety commitments are treated as material statements that need an 8-K.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, August 2030 (generation 45)
Post-IPO lock-up and Reg FD constraints matter: Reg FD limits selective disclosure, but it does not bar public policy advocacy. It would be realistic to model the post-listing regime as loosening voice through public, broadly disseminated channels while still constraining private briefings to investors or selected parties.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, September 2030 (generation 46)
Powered reruns of a near-miss are a distinct channel from first attempts. Once a pre-registered run comes back underpowered, deciders usually accept a rerun with a stated power calculation more readily than a new method. It would be realistic to model "replicate at power" as a lower-friction follow-up whose result is treated as decisive.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, October 2030 (generation 47)
Splitting an action into a vetoed sub-step and a non-vetoed remainder, for example "validate items" versus "fund a test of a named competitor," is a realistic way through a counsel objection. It would help if outcome text said which sub-step carried the veto, so the rest can go ahead without it.

## du-2026-10-01-opus55-no-adversary-fixed50, November 2030 (generation 48)
In the final acting month, it would be useful to know which actions the simulator counts as "in effect on the deadline" versus "pending". That would let players prioritise things that complete within the month, such as merged code or a published advisory, over things that only start, such as onboarding or a drafted resolution.
