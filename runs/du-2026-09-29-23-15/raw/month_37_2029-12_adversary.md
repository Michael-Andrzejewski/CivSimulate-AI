<research_summary>
Real-world evidence supports three points. First, out-of-band callback verification is already the security industry's main recommended defence against voice-clone fraud. Research on audio-deepfake detection shows detectors generalise poorly to unseen transformations such as codec changes. So the codec-augmentation retrain may not close the gap. Anthropic's "recommended rule" also risks looking like old advice relabelled as a new fix, and publishing the postmortem creates legal exposure. Second, SecureBio's testing keeps getting harder (VCT-v2 was redesigned to resist shortcuts) and it is protective of its independence. Given four prior slips and two-sided criteria, a fail or a partial result on the over-refusal side is a real possibility. Third, OpenAI already has a $250M worker fund, and it drew "not proportionate to the scale" criticism. Unions increasingly want worker-designed training rather than training designed by tech companies. So an Anthropic $250M tranche could be read as copying OpenAI and as too small. Finally, CAISI has a mandate to aggregate incident data but little money or scope. Congressional friction over CAISI's openness continues, which makes a supplemental filing that narrows the burden onto deployers of open models a new target for hosting platforms.
