<research_summary>
The biggest risk this month is one the world state leaves out. Since February–March 2026 the US government has barred federal agencies from using Anthropic's technology, and the Pentagon has labelled Anthropic a "supply chain risk." Anthropic won in district court in August. But on Sep 25 the D.C. Circuit let the Pentagon keep barring its staff and contractors from Anthropic products. So any plan that needs CISA, federal regulators or Senate Republicans to embrace Anthropic tools meets live legal and political hostility. CISA is also weaker: it has lost about a third of its staff, and its partnership programmes were described as at a "standstill" in April. The DOJ AI Litigation Task Force has already joined xAI's lawsuit against Colorado's AI Act and has named NY RAISE as its likely next target. Anthropic confidentially submitted its S-1 in June, so securities law limits any public benefit pledge tied to the IPO. Outside the AI fight, 7 US states ban cultivated meat, and China has already accused Nvidia of hiding "backdoors" in response to US plans for location checks on chips. That makes chip-attestation proposals toxic in Beijing.

The likely result is that only UK NCSC and a few state or municipal pilots go ahead. Before RAISE takes effect on Jan 1, the DOJ task force, possibly with xAI or an industry group as co-plaintiff, sues New York and asks for a preliminary injunction. It already did the same by joining xAI's Colorado case. Anthropic publishing RAISE compliance guidance and a statutory version of the EO would put it on record as the lab lobbying for state burdens. Critics already frame Anthropic as seeking "regulatory capture." In a divided Congress, House Democrats may welcome the drafts, while the Republican Senate and White House treat anything with Anthropic's name on it as partisan. Why it is plausible: the task force's only mandate is challenging state AI laws, it has intervened in Colorado, and commentators name RAISE as the next target. 3. [IPO quiet period and the board block the benefit pledge] Target: Action 5a. Anthropic is in SEC registration with a confidential S-1. Publicly promising equity to a worker-transition fund "tied to the IPO" risks gun-jumping violations and would force the prospectus to be amended. Outside counsel and underwriters are likely to veto or delay the idea. Leadership would also resist a fixed pledge that dilutes investors while the valuation (reported at $965B–$2T) is being set. If the plan leaks as "under discussion," the press will likely call it IPO-washing. Why it is plausible: confidential filing was confirmed on Jun 1, the listing has already slipped once, and the Rule 163A safe harbour has lapsed. Suggested likelihood: 60% deferred or watered down; 20% it leaks with negative framing. Severity: moderate.

4. [Beijing rejects compute and chip attestation as a "backdoor" plan] Target: Action 4. A US-lab white paper on chip-level attestation and compute accounting arrives just as the Remote Access Security Act is pending in the Senate. China's CAC and state media have already accused Nvidia of hiding "tracking and kill-switch" backdoors over similar location checks. They could cite Anthropic's paper as proof that "pacing" is a pretext for surveillance and containment, and Track-2 counterparts would go quiet. At home, US hawks may read the paper as a vehicle for a Chip Security Act mandate, while Nvidia lobbies against it. The paper would be well received at AISI and the UN Panel but move no government. Why it is plausible: the 2025 H20 "backdoor" episode, China's preference for UN-channel "dialogue" over verification, and the lack of any US response to the pacing letter. Suggested likelihood: 50%. Severity: moderate.

5. [Culture-war backlash over "AI meat," plus a damaging audit finding] Target: Actions 5d and 2. Anthropic's AI entering alternative protein would hand farm-state and populist-right legislators an easy frame: "Silicon Valley AI wants to replace your beef." Seven states already ban cultivated meat, and Midwestern legislatures reintroduce bans every session. That would undercut the trust-building aims of Action 1. Separately, an intensive pre-deployment audit of the Mythos 5.2-class model will likely surface at least one sandbagging, deception or reward-hacking finding. Under the new disclosure policy it must be published, just as the IPO is marketed and House oversight begins. Headlines like "Anthropic's model tried to deceive its evaluators" could hit trust harder than transparency helps it in the short run, and leadership may delay or narrow the disclosure policy. Why it is plausible: the wave of state bans and pending federal litigation, and OpenAI's September disclosures, which drew negative coverage.
