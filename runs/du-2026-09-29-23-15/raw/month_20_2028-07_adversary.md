<research_summary>
Real-world precedent points to delays rather than dramatic failures this month. Law enforcement routinely asks companies to delay disclosure, and in practice police prefer "until charged or closed" over a fixed date. Under the FBI/DOJ SEC framework, delays run in 30-day extensions with no firm ceiling, so Pennsylvania State Police are unlikely to accept a 120-day cap or let a third-party custodian hold case findings. Charitable-trust law (no private inurement, trustee oversight) gives charity counsel room to resist any seat where an interested federation holds a veto, so the "independent expert with a federation veto" design may simply move the objection. The Census Bureau's differential-privacy rollout shows how much a "standard" noise parameter varies (ε ended up far above initial demos, around 19–40, and was heavily criticised). Pointing to a "Census-style ε" therefore won't settle the parameter question and may invite a new privacy-vs-accuracy fight. AISI's publication record, including its December 2025 Frontier AI Trends report, shows it both publishes and builds automated jailbreaks. Its July re-run could therefore surface new bypasses, and it is unlikely to publish within the month. Most murine thigh-model programmes for Klebsiella leads fail to reach ≥2 log10 kill at tolerable doses, so a miss on the readout is a realistic base-rate outcome.
