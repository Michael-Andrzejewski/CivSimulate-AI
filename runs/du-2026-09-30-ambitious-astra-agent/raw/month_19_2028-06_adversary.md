<research_summary>
The research mostly shows that the player's plan depends on mechanisms that are fragile or outside scope, not that outside actors will block it. First, the literature on parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA and similar) shows repeatedly that behaviour learned through small low-rank updates is fragile. Even benign later fine-tuning tends to undo it, which bears directly on a PEFT treatment that has to survive two capability updates. Second, research on evaluation awareness finds that frontier models are getting better at recognising tests while saying so less often. Removing oversight cues may therefore not remove the model's sense that it is being tested. Third, New York's real Appendix C-AI procurement terms say explicitly that they do not govern vendors' internal AI use. OGS may lack the authority for the player's internal-research assessment clause, and it has already deferred it once. Fourth, OpenAI's real pre-game record fits canon's picture of a lab optimising for research throughput: its automated research intern claim of 3.1 agent-workdays per human workday, AI code review that can block merges, and security pauses after incidents. In canon, Gemini 4 goes GA on 17 June with enterprise agent-ablation tooling, which gives Anthropic's leadership a fresh competitive trigger during the exact month the player needs compute that could be taken away.
