Real-world evidence points to five main risks this month. **First, the statistics.** At n=1,000 and a rate near 4%, the 95% confidence interval is about ±1.2pp. To get the upper bound to 4.6% or below, the point estimate has to fall to about 3.4%, which is a big drop from 4.3%. Current research on refusal calibration (BioTIER, boundary-aware refusal work) shows that pushing bio refusals down usually trades off against other behaviours. **Second, the Frontier Model Forum.** Google, OpenAI and Anthropic have been building a separate FINRA-style self-regulatory body, "SAFA", with Hassabis as the original proposer. That gives OpenAI and GDM a ready reason to send Anthropic's rule there instead of adopting it. Lawfare and FMF material also show antitrust counsel are nervous about competitors agreeing shared numeric thresholds. **Third, severance.** McLaren Macomb is still live precedent, even though the NLRB General Counsel has rescinded its guidance and a new Board looks set to reverse it. So telling workers "no confidentiality" is legally sound, but it changes the story from "secret number" to "which number," and lets the federation compile the worst cases. **Fourth, the lame duck.** In past lame-duck sessions preemption has been pushed through the NDAA, and the White House leans towards OpenAI and xAI. Any levy modelling Anthropic offers will be read through the "windfall-tax company's homework" frame whoever wins.
