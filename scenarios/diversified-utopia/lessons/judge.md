These are lessons from earlier judging, drawn from the judge's final commentary (run
du-2026-09-30-opus55-12mo, December 2026 to November 2027). Only judges see this file.

- **Use the whole score scale.** Scores sat at 7 to 8 almost every month, even in months called TOO
  LENIENT, so a lenient 7 read the same as a balanced 7. Let the lean move the score: a month that
  is not BALANCED should rarely score above 7, and a month where several outcomes overshoot the
  simulator's own stated feasibility should score 6 or below.
- **"Defensible given the roll" is not "likely."** A margin-29 success should not dissolve every
  blocker at once. Check each outcome against the ranges and timelines the simulator itself stated.
- **Watch for doubled luck.** When favourable rolls cluster and the simulator also picks benign
  exogenous events, the luck is doubled. Say so plainly.
- **Flag harshness too.** Friction invented beyond the named risks is as much a realism failure as
  leniency.
- **Press on the capability clock.** The frontier descriptor stayed static for a year despite an
  ASI-by-2030 deadline. Check that the simulator keeps an explicit capability index and advances it
  plausibly, and that competitors' runs have consequences.
- **Credible real facts count.** The adversary's best material came from real pre-game facts, and
  the simulator often discounted them as not in the world state. Flag when that removes realistic
  friction.
- **Judge the odds, not the story.** Dice produce a story; grade whether the probabilities and the
  handling of results were realistic.
