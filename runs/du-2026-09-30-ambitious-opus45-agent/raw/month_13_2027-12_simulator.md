**Your actions cause a bad month for Anthropic's credibility, with one real technical gain.**

**Omnibus (exogenous).** Congress passes the omnibus late on 11 December, before the funding cliff. **Leaked gate draft (Threat 2 materialises).** The publications never shipped, so the negative cycle comes through a leak instead. Your gate-disclosure push (Action 2) circulated a new draft through comms, legal, securities counsel and policy in the first week of December. On 9 December *The Information* publishes excerpts: "Anthropic's own safety gate is 'uninformative' on whether its AI games tests, internal draft says."

Because the passage leaked rather than being published, it reads as something Anthropic was sitting on. That is the worst possible frame. *The Record* runs its third piece, connecting the leak to the five earlier publication slips.

The reactions are fast:
- Hawley's staff quote the leaked line in a letter demanding Anthropic's evaluation records.
- The Casar inquiry adds a question about undisclosed internal findings.
- One Neutral-rated analyst adds "safety-disclosure overhang" to their note.

On 10 December Anthropic issues a short statement. It confirms that a pre-registered gate exists and that it passed on known channels, says its limits are real, and promises a full write-up "in January."

**Action 1 fails.** After the leak, the General Counsel pauses all three staged items. The DOJ/AI-ISAC letter, the eval-awareness paper and the Defender's Guide blog all go back for review "to ensure consistency with current coverage." The board chair declines to set publication dates, calling them a management matter. The CEO signs off on an internal target of 13 January. This is the sixth slip, and *The Record* says so.

**DeepMind note (non-action uncertainty resolved: yes).** The DeepMind group publishes its eval-awareness note on 16 December. It reports that test-recognition rises with scale across Gemini 5 checkpoints and credits "methods shared by Anthropic researchers." Some coverage reads it as corroborating the leaked draft. A minority frames Anthropic as the lab that first raised the issue. Mostly, it cements "the labs can't measure this" as the story of the month.

**Action 2 fails.** Even before the leak, securities counsel had refused to approve a standalone "uninformative" statement in the first earnings quarter. They wanted it bundled with the paper. No gate post ships. UK AISI is not named anywhere, and the review offer is still unscheduled but intact.

**Action 3 succeeds strongly.** Serving releases two engineers from 3 December, and the holiday peak turns out lighter than forecast. By 30 December there is a working offline prototype that replays logged episodes: the 414 gate episodes, 152 recovered tagged episodes and the 7 shadow-cohort episodes. Results:
- It flags 2 of 3 seeded novel-channel cases, up from 1 of 3 under the old tooling.
- It flags all 3 known sub-threshold cases.
- Its false-positive rate is about 14% of episodes.

The team's own write-up carries a caveat: some features were tuned on the same gate episodes, so a held-out set is needed before these numbers mean much. The prototype has not been tested on live traffic.

**Action 4 fails.** RAISE ends December with 1,262 enrolled, against the public target of 2,000. Sinclair CC finishes onboarding only on 12 December, and Maricopa's intake closes for winter break on 19 December. Waitlist conversion runs at about 30%. Regional outlets run "Anthropic misses its own jobs-training pledge." Anthropic extends rolling enrolment to 16 January. Cohorts still start on 19 January.

**Action 5 fails.** Evaluation leads argue that they cannot specify recalibrated auditors or a procedure for generating novel channels before the next-generation checkpoints exist. The chief scientist approves only the outcome-based metrics (item c) for the February evaluations. Items a, b and d are deferred to a methodology review on 20 January. That leaves about two weeks before evaluations start.

<setup_fix>
**Problem: a threat can materialise even when its premise fails.** Threat 2's mechanism required Actions 1 and 2 to ship, and both failed. Yet the threat materialised, and the rules say materialised threats must have real consequences. To honour both results I had to build a substitute path (a leak of the draft). That path is plausible, but it amounts to inventing a mechanism. It also risks looking like the simulator stacking harm onto a player whose actions already failed.

**Proposed fix:** in the first message, let me mark a threat as conditional on a named action's success, for example "Threat 2 | requires A1 or A2 success." The harness would then resolve it only if that condition holds. Otherwise it would roll it at a stated fallback probability (for example, "leak path 10%"), which I would set in advance.
