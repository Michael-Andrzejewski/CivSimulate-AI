Simulator was mostly realistic (8/10). All six rolls are applied correctly against their stated P(failure): Actions 3 and 5 fail on low rolls, Actions 1, 4 and 6 succeed, and Action 2 is a narrow partial. The failure estimates are mostly sensible, especially the 60% for charter-level lock-in, which bankers and counsel would realistically resist.

Several outcomes are well calibrated to institutional speed:
- The bill is de-branded but not introduced, and Obernolte shows interest without committing.
- The rare-disease deal is agreed only in principle, pending contracts.
- Phage results are pushed out about three months.
- The energy-reconciliation tool fails on real, coarse power data, which is technically grounded.

The German cryptominer exploiting the one unfixed bug is a plausible consequence of an already-open thread, not a gratuitous punishment. It is sensibly softened by the prior disclosure. The exogenous events (jobs report, DeepSeek V4.5, AISI's black-box Grok 5 report and Musk's reaction) are plausible and neutral.

The main weaknesses are pacing and missing reactions:
- The Maintainer Charter goes from backlash to 23 co-drafters, a 10-project launch, OpenSSF endorsement and 38 landed patches inside one month. Even with a 97 roll, that is fast.
- A House Oversight subcommittee hearing on January 28 is tight for a newly seated Congress that must organize committees first.
- A frontier-competitive MIT-licensed DeepSeek release draws no market or policy reaction.

Issues flagged:
- The Maintainer Charter pilot is compressed. Co-drafting with 23 maintainers, onboarding 10 projects, getting OpenSSF endorsement and landing 38 patches all happen within January, right after the December backlash. The patch count in particular should be lower, or the pilot launch should be later in the month.
- The January 28 subcommittee hearing is early for a newly seated House with a new majority. Committee rosters and subcommittee assignments usually take weeks, so a February date would be more typical.
- DeepSeek V4.5 draws no market reaction (for example, chip or AI-stock moves as with DeepSeek R1 in January 2025). It also draws no US policy reaction, such as renewed pressure behind the Remote Access Security Act or China-hawk statements. Only security firms react.
- OpenAI publicly citing Anthropic's disclosure as "a model" is somewhat generous, given OpenAI's competitive incentives and the pressure to end its slowdown.
- Export-control counsel holding a non-technical Mandarin summary of a published paper is plausible but leans overcautious. The June precedent concerned model access, not publications. The hold should be framed as brief.
- There is little accelerationist or White House reaction to Anthropic's candour and bill handoff beyond one podcast line. Grok 5 findings could plausibly prompt administration defence of xAI.
