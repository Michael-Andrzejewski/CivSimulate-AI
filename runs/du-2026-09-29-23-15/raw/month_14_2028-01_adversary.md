<research_summary>
There are four real constraints behind this month's threats. First, SEC Rule 10b5-1 has required a 90–120 day cooling-off period for directors and officers since the 2022 amendments. Any trading plan adopted in January 2028 therefore cannot trade until April or May. Plans that already exist were probably adopted during the Mythos hold, which plaintiffs could use. Second, lockup expiries typically bring a 1–3% fall, about 60% of stocks decline around them, and there are tail cases like Uber's 40%. Third, CAISI really is tiny: 20–30 staff and about $15M in FY2026, against analyst calls for $84M or more. That makes it fragile as a custodian or confidential recipient, and it is sensitive to looking captured by a lab. Fourth, Senate Commerce Chair Cruz rejects mandatory testing. He, Thune and Klobuchar clashed with Anthropic in July 2026 when Anthropic "refused to play ball" on their bill, so Republican chairs have an existing grievance and may not take an "even-handed" offer as neutral. Taken together, the biggest risks this month are that securities law turns the labour plan into a broken promise, that partisan actors turn the congressional offer into a weapon, and that the first real-world exposure of Mythos 6 produces a trigger event or leak.
