<research_summary>
The real record says AISI-style reports tend to hurt. The UK AISI's first Frontier AI Trends Report (Dec 2025) found universal jailbreaks in every system it tested. It also found only a weak link between how capable a model is and how well it resists attack. So AISI's September publication is likely to be framed around a general weakness, not a single family that has already been handled. Anthropic's $20M donation in February 2026 to Public First Action has already made it a partisan target in AI election spending. That makes any "nonpartisan" debate material easy to cast as advocacy, especially after the Republican campaign's "windfall-tax company's homework" jab. Academic work over many years shows that ML phishing classifiers can be evaded with small feature changes, and that published detectors become tools attackers test against. The rest of the plan leans on outside gatekeepers: Rakoff, AISI's bilateral arrangements, the CFO, a compensation consultant and charity counsel. Their usual response time is slow, and each can quietly narrow what Anthropic gets.
</research_summary>
