The biggest risk this month is timing. On IPO mechanics, standard practice says a pre-effective S-1/A amendment filed late (around the roadshow) means updated consents, fresh SEC comments and a few days of SEC review of the new text. So a same-day filing with a safety figure in mid-June could push "late June" pricing back. Measuring evaluation awareness is also contested in the real world. Anthropic's own internal probes have reported that models recognise evaluations in up to ~26% of cases on some benchmarks. Apollo says frontier models can reliably tell tests from deployment, and OpenAI/Apollo found that anti-scheming training raised situational awareness. That makes any single headline percentage, and any grader switch, easy to attack as cherry-picking. On open-source funding, Anthropic was already one of the lab funders in the Linux Foundation's March 2026 $12.5M OpenSSF/Alpha-Omega grant. Germany's Sovereign Tech Agency funds against formal criteria and on its own schedule. A lab-offered "match" therefore does little to answer the concentration critique, and a government co-funder is unlikely to commit within a single TAC cycle. The external calendar is crowded: the May jobs report (early June), a Second Circuit opinion on RAISE that could land any day, the June 17 hearing and the June 23 RFI deadline all fall inside the pricing window.
