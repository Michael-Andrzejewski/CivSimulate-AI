<research_summary>
The biggest real-world risk is the Pentagon's "supply chain risk" designation of Anthropic. It is the first such label ever given to a US company. On Sep 25, 2026 the D.C. Circuit upheld it 2-1, while a California district court called it unlawful retaliation. That leaves Anthropic shut out of DoD contracts and politically exposed in the administration whose executive order, Commerce department and CAISI the player now wants to work through. David Sacks's attacks have escalated. They go beyond "regulatory capture": in September he said Anthropic and OpenAI wanted an antitrust exemption to form a "cartel," and that METR is "intertwined with Anthropic's investors and staff." Several conservative outlets picked this up. So any Anthropic tooling placed inside competitors' evaluations, and any governance rule that names METR, is a ready target. CAISI is underfunded (roughly a tenth of UK AISI's budget), and senators are already pressing it on transparency. Competitor labs have a history of fighting over access for evaluation purposes: Anthropic cut off OpenAI's Claude access in Aug 2025. On labour, states are already moving on their own (CT AI disclosure in WARN notices since Oct 1, 2026, IL SB3571, CA SB951), so a "neutral" Anthropic memo arrives late and will be read as industry lobbying. Wet-lab validation of AI-designed antimicrobials still fails at high rates.
</research_summary>

Wells Fargo-type enterprise customers may object if the index makes their automation choices easy to identify by sector, and privacy advocates may question using conversation telemetry to map job loss. Getting a signed state workforce-agency or community-college partner within about two weeks is unlikely, because public-sector procurement and data-sharing reviews take months. The launch then either slips or goes out without a partner, which undermines its credibility. The evidence memo to state legislators arrives after CT, IL and NY have already acted on AI disclosure in WARN notices and CA SB951 has been sent to the governor. Legislators will likely see it as industry positioning, not neutral evidence. Why it is plausible: states are already legislating on this, and the Economic Index's methods rely on sampled conversation telemetry. 4. [The goodness pilot misses again: calibration bar, compute contention, Goodhart] Target: Action 1. Building a 400-item human-labelled gold set for long-horizon consent judgements and pushing judge agreement from about 60% to over 85% in two weeks is very ambitious. Labellers will disagree on items like "would the principal endorse this on reflection," and breaking the judgement into a checklist tends to raise agreement by rewarding surface compliance. The compute request competes with evaluations of the frontier successor and with Anthropic's own analysis of GPT-6 and Gemini 4, which lost the pilot its baseline in December. The likeliest outcome is a technically clean null result, or a small positive effect confined to the checklist, which the research leads read as "retire it." Why it is plausible: last month's actual failure mode, general evaluator overload, and the well-known difficulty of reaching agreement among LLM judges on subjective long-horizon criteria. Sources: https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168414 ; https://ifp.org/funding-for-caisi/. Suggested likelihood: 45% that the calibration targets are missed or the matched comparison is again underpowered, and 30% that it finishes but returns a null result. Severity: moderate.

5. [LTBT waters down the minimal ask, and wet-lab hits fail] Target: Actions 5 and 6. IPO counsel will likely resist putting "release decisions are not governed by financing timelines" into risk-factor disclosures, because it creates securities-liability exposure if a later release looks commercially timed. The LTBT may record only non-binding minutes. Requiring evaluations from "two of AISI/CAISI/METR/Apollo" also puts METR, which Sacks says is not independent, inside a public commitment. Separately, the first wet-lab results in February and March may show that the AI-designed anti-Klebsiella phage-derived compounds lose activity in serum, fail on cytotoxicity, or quickly select for resistance, which is the normal attrition for early antimicrobial hits. The preprint would be delayed or reduced to a "negative/partial" data release. Why it is plausible: base rates for preclinical attrition, and the general caution of a company preparing an IPO.
