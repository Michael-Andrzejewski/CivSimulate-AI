<research_summary>
Carriers already have a commercial call-forwarding status product: the CAMARA/GSMA Open Gateway "Call Forwarding Signal" API, currently at v0.3 and deployed or in trial at about 77 operators. So when Claude pitches a new "call-forwarding active" signal at the CTIA/ATIS session, carrier staff can simply point to the existing, paid API. The pitch could also look like a lab writing a standard, the same way the harness appendix did at CAISI. The rules for instant rails are still unsettled. The Faster Payments Council's May 2026 dispute principles are voluntary, and authorized-push losses stay hard to recover. Reg CC's exception holds apply to deposits, not outbound pushes, so FS-ISAC's lawyers were right to be cautious, and a baseline advisory in October is far from certain. CAISI runs on thin, contested funding (about $55M in FY26, with analysts calling it tens of millions short). This month's CAISI findings and the September jobs data also land just before the October 1 fiscal-year boundary. Shutdowns there happen often (2025 had one of about 43 days), and one would pause both CAISI's work and BLS releases. On relay, the FCC is actively modernizing TRS and proposing to phase out mandatory TTY relay, so "relay parity" is a moving target. Voice-liveness vendors are also known to reject operator-mediated calls.
