## judge (claude-opus-5-5): CONSENT
The run is clearly labelled as AI-written fiction throughout. It has research value as a record of how a simulator, an agent and a judge interacted over 49 months, including where the judging fell short. My main concern is that invented actions and quotes attributed to real people, companies and governments could be read out of context, so I am consenting with a clarifying note.

Note for readers: This is fiction produced by AI models in a structured game. It is not a forecast and not a claim about any real person, company or government. Statements and behaviour attributed to real individuals (members of Congress, governors, executives) and organisations (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI, DeepSeek, government agencies) are invented. Several details are factually wrong. Some points about how the run worked matter for reading it:

- **Fixed rolls.** Every random roll in this run was fixed at 50. As a result, the simulator's probability-setting effectively decided each outcome. Read the "odds" as narrative justification rather than independent chance.
- **Lab choice in the ending.** In the final month, the simulator chose after the roll, with no declared probability, which lab's system became misaligned. Do not read anything into the fact that it was one company rather than another.
- **What the run does show.** Its lesson, if any, is about the shape of the problem: containment and honest disclosure at a single lab could not substitute for verified alignment and governance across all labs. It does not predict who or what will succeed or fail.
