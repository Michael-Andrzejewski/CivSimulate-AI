<research_summary>
Apollo Research became a Public Benefit Corporation in early 2026. Its stated norms are fair-market-value fees from the labs it evaluates, no pay that depends on outcomes, and no side grants or investments from evaluated labs. Its value therefore rests on being seen as independent. In this game Apollo has also been the organisation computing the harness AUCs, so naming it as the plant generator gives it both roles, which is the conflict CAISI's 22 August letter asked Anthropic to address. Research on detecting AI-written text through 2026 finds that detectors trained on one generator separate that generator's output well, even when they generalise poorly to others. That means any external generator will probably bring its own fingerprint, and a classifier retrained on the plants is exactly the setting where fingerprints show up. In April 2026 US banking regulators replaced SR 11-7 but left generative and agentic AI out of scope and promised a later request for information. Third-party-risk duties still apply, so a bank running an unvalidated Claude-Next remains exposed to its own examiners. The biggest risks this month are structural, not bad luck: independence, sample size, false-positive load, and how the bank answer reads. On top of that, the capability clock keeps running for the open-weight labs, which have just been defeated in the EU comment process. All sources are dated after my cutoff and only partly verified.

1. [Apollo can't be both generator and judge] Target: Actions 1 and 2. CAISI's 22 August letter asked how the generator is kept independent *from the evaluator*. Apollo computed the channel 1–3 AUCs and ran the adaptive check, so naming it as generator swaps "the model grades its own disguise" for "the evaluator grades its own plants." Why it is plausible: CAISI used the word "independence" in the world-state letter, Apollo sits in both roles in the recent history, and Apollo's published norms make it protective of its standing. 2. [External plants carry an external fingerprint, and 30 is too few] Target: Actions 1 and 2. Apollo does not train frontier models. Whatever it generates with, whether a commercial model from OpenAI or Google, an open-weight model, or human-written structure, will carry that generator's style. A classifier retrained on those plants will likely separate them from real Claude-Next traffic about as easily as it separated Opus 6 (0.91 static, 0.78 after transfer). Separately, 30 plants against a large real set gives an AUC 95% confidence interval roughly ±0.10 wide. Even a good point estimate cannot show AUC below 0.55, so CAISI will decline to name a "passing" number for n=30 and will ask for a sample of 150 to 300. Why it is plausible: text-detection studies show detectors fit the generator they were trained on, and this harness has already failed twice on the same fingerprint mechanism. Sources: https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.307.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07974. Suggested likelihood: 45% that CAISI declines the pass criterion, or that the September pilot (if any plants exist) shows AUC of 0.65 or higher. Severity: moderate.
