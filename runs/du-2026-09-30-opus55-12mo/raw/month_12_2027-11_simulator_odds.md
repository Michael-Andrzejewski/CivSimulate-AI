**Context for the frontier picture.** GDM's roughly 1e28 run finished around 25 October. That model, once post-trained, probably sits at or slightly above Anthropic's internal successor. Anthropic's lead should now be treated as parity rather than an edge. Post-training plus the EO's 30-day window makes any public GDM preview before early December unlikely, though not impossible. The capability trajectory is unchanged:
- about 1.5 GW online for Anthropic, with competitors comparable;
- multi-day autonomous SWE;
- a median of about Q3 2028 for more than 90% automated research engineering;
- this keeps ASI-level capability plausibly on track for late 2030.

**Action 1 (checkpoints and deployment gate).**
- Checkpoints 6 and 7, and the honeypot pre-registration, are routine. The team has run five clean checkpoints.
- The new element is the published successor deployment gate. It commits Anthropic to UK AISI parallel access and to METR/Redwood sign-off as release conditions. Leadership must approve it, commercial teams will resist a self-imposed delay while GDM is at parity, and the GC's office is primed to fight again.
- The White House and Buist risks are modelled in Threat 2, so they are not double-counted here.
- Execution risks are a slip in checkpoint 7's timing and leadership asking for edits to the gate text.

**Action 2 (Commerce and Congress technical package).**
- Sending a letter to BIS is easy.
- Publishing agentic-intrusion uplift numbers for open-weight models is itself dual-use. Threat-intel and counsel review, plus the CISA and Mandiant sharing agreements, may strip indicators or delay release.
- Anchoring the political reaction is the hard part, and "no stance" framing tends not to hold for long.
- The capture framing belongs to Threat 3.

**Action 3 (scaling the defender kit).**
- Going from 212 to 500 KYC-verified organisations in 30 days needs new reviewer hiring or training. Per the judge's feedback, the enrolment ramp should be gradual.
- WaterISAC and the state hospital associations each need MOU and legal review, which takes weeks.
- Publishing the detection signatures and hardening checklists openly is easy.
- The IGSC package is feasible.
- The likely outcome is partial: roughly 330 to 420 organisations.

**Action 4 (drift interventions and Q4 readout).**
- Pre-announcing what a miss means is easy.
- A readout on 25 November can't include confirmer outputs for checkpoints 6 and 7, since those reviews take weeks. It will therefore be partial by construction.
- Threat 1, if it materialises, adds a caveat.
- The drift result itself arrives in December.

**Action 5 (jobs benefits).**
- Stanford controls its own publication timing.
- Second-state procurement cannot close within a month. At best, an offer is made and a board expresses interest.
- A Tri-County free tier needs agreements with the Ohio and Indiana workforce boards, and the politics are touchy.
- Utah continuing is easy.

**Action 6 (international evidence submissions).**
- Submitting to the UN panel and the International Network's technical track, and publishing under CC-BY, are within Anthropic's control.
- Uptake and citation are slow.
- A minor risk: publishing the protocol while it sits with DSIT for clearance irritates DSIT. "Foreign back door" critics may seize on the UN submission.
