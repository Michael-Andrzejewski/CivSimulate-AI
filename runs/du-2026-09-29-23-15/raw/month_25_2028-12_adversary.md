<research_summary>
Real-world precedent shows that lame-duck AI preemption riders keep coming back and keep failing. The FY26 NDAA dropped preemption after bipartisan resistance from HASC Chair Rogers and SASC Ranking Member Reed. Republicans then said they were "looking at other" vehicles, and the Trump preemption executive order plus the DOJ task force stayed in place. So a December 2028 lame-duck rider is possible but more likely to fail than pass, while executive and litigation pressure on state laws continues until Jan 20. That matters with RAISE en banc argument due in January. Adversarial-ML research (the GuardPhish and PhishLang work) confirms that text classifiers get evaded by fine-tuned LLM lures in shifted registers. Only adversarial retraining combined with non-text signals holds up, which fits the 88% to 61% slide in the world state and suggests behavioural-signal integration will lift recovery only partly. Several things converge on the Dec 14 board meeting with no margin: a divided board, antitrust counsel's caution, a CFO who is already hostile, and AISI reading four days later. Institutional inertia (counsel, vendors, the holidays) is the dominant failure mode this month, not exotic events.
</research_summary>
