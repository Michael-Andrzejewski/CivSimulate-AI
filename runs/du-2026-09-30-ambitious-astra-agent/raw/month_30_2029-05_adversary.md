<research_summary>
Four findings bear on this month's plan.

1. **A near-frontier open base means a Chinese model.** The most capable open-weight bases are Chinese: Qwen, DeepSeek and Kimi, with Qwen alone at about 151k Hugging Face derivatives. Picking a base "by measured research capability" therefore probably means using one of them. That happens in the same month as a Senate Commerce hearing on open-weight agents called after Cascade Valley, and Anthropic has publicly opposed open-weight releases and accused DeepSeek of distillation.
2. **Proposed liability would reach anyone who modifies a model.** The real Durbin–Hawley AI LEAD Act treats anyone who "substantially modifies" a model as a developer, and it names open-weight models. Anthropic engineers packaging fixes for a V5.5 scaffold and supporting its fine-tunes would carry exactly that exposure, which counsel will notice.
3. **The antitrust signal on lab coordination is split.** DOJ (Woodward, Sep 2026) was open to safety coordination, but FTC Chair Ferguson was sceptical of labs that ask for both rules and antitrust relief. *Buist v. Anthropic* is still pending in canon. A cross-lab compact that argues for "reciprocal restrictions on expanding autonomous AI R&D" looks like an output restraint.
4. **Cheap fine-tuning results may not hold up.** A 2026 preprint says subliminal-transfer effects are a LoRA artefact that disappears under full fine-tuning, though its authors flag that some claims are being revised. Three compute points will probably force low-rank adaptation on a near-frontier base, so any effect it finds may not survive the planned ordinary follow-on update.

Meanwhile the capability clock keeps running: a Gemini 5 preview is due in Q2 and OpenAI's new run is under way.
