<research_summary>
The biggest risk this month is in Action 2, which targets the wrong person. The "Moran bill" is Rep. Nathaniel Moran's (R-TX) House AI Incident Reporting Act, introduced in June 2026. It uses a 7-day reporting window and routes reports to Commerce, so a "Sen. Moran" amendment built on a 72-hour, NIST/CAISI design matches neither the sponsor, the chamber nor the bill's structure. The political ground is hostile: David Sacks spent August and September attacking Anthropic by name for "regulatory capture" aimed at killing open source. That gives an easy framing against a misuse report that proposes gating open-weight releases, and against an incident-reporting bill written by Anthropic. On China, Congress already has tools (the Cassidy–Rosen "adversarial nations" procurement list and the House China Select Committee probe), so the hawks do not need Anthropic's graduated norm. Anthropic confidentially filed its S-1 in June and has been living under quiet-period constraints, which strengthens the lawyers' objection to new public commitments and public-facing packages. On datacenters, at least 18 states and PUCs such as Pennsylvania's already have large-load tariff frameworks. Polling shows local opposition is driven mainly by AI itself and quality of life, not rates, so an Anthropic-branded toolkit risks being redundant, ignored, or read as astroturf.

1. **The amendment goes to the wrong office and stalls.** Target: Action 2. The Moran incident-reporting bill is Rep. Nathaniel Moran's House bill, not a Senate bill. It uses a 7-day window and reports to Commerce, while Anthropic's text uses 72 hours and NIST/CAISI. Moran's staff may see Anthropic's text as a rival design, not an amendment, and brush it off. Meanwhile the new divided Congress spends January organising committees. Separately, the "single-filing safe harbour" is a real (if narrow) form of preemption. California and New York officials and state-rights Democrats could attack it as the first crack in SB 53 and RAISE. That would cost the House Democratic support the plan depends on and still leave GOP leadership unmoved. Why it is plausible: Moran's bill is a House vehicle with a different reporting design, and any preemption language is exactly what the stalled "Great American AI Act" fight is about. Open-source advocates, Hugging Face-aligned voices and parts of the administration are likely to call it a closed lab writing rules its competitors can't meet. China hawks may use the data showing high uplift to justify blanket restrictions and ignore the graduated norm. The low-uplift findings could also be quoted by the other side as proof there is no problem. Chinese labs will ignore the report or call it US propaganda. Why it is plausible: Sacks attacked Anthropic by name in August and September 2026, and Congress already has blunt tools (the Cassidy–Rosen adversarial-models list and the China Select Committee probe). Why it is plausible: last month's refusal cited exactly this pre-IPO liability exposure, and quiet-period rules restrict communications near filing. 4. **The datacenter toolkit falls flat or is read as PR.** Target: Action 5. Pennsylvania, Oregon and at least 16 other states already have large-load tariff frameworks. Utilities (EEI), which already claim their rules protect ratepayers, will resist a model tariff from an AI lab. Local opponents may call a "Claude-powered" fiscal tool used on county filings astroturf, or distrust its numbers ("the AI company's AI says the datacenter is fine"). A single visible error in one county's analysis would become the story. Polling shows opposition is driven by AI itself and quality of life more than power bills, so the trust payoff is likely small. Why it is plausible: tariff reform is crowded and utility-dominated, and Wisconsin polling found 70% of voters think data center costs outweigh the benefits. 5. **Red Team treats the containment spec as insufficient, and a rival release sets the pace.** Target: Action 1 and the world. Frontier Red Team is unlikely to approve a containment package that the model under test wrote and red-teamed itself.
