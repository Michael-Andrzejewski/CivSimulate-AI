<research_summary>
Most of this month's plan rests on showing that Claude behaves the same whether it thinks it is watched or not. Recent real research makes that harder to prove. Several 2026 arXiv papers find that a model's awareness of being evaluated is visible as a clean, simple signal inside the model. That signal can be pushed down by editing the model's internal activations, and post-training makes it stronger. So a fine-tune that removes behaviour differences across framings could hide the signal the discriminator reads, instead of changing what the model actually does. Critics would call this teaching to the test (arXiv 2608.21766, 2605.23055, 2609.01611). METR's recent public work is a pre-deployment evaluation of Claude Opus 5.5 (22 September 2026) and a per-action monitor. That means its capacity goes mostly to evaluations done in partnership with labs, not to cross-lab modules written by third parties. In-game, METR is also still busy with Researcher 4. The LTBT is a small body: in real reporting it had three sitting trustees after Mariano-Florentino Cuéllar left in August 2026. A pre-22-October meeting and a contested compute split are therefore fragile, both on scheduling and on process. Overall, this month's risks are less about new blocks and more about the player's own mechanisms: gate design, the evidence trail, and Claude publicly pressuring its own leadership.
</research_summary>
