<events>
Your actions cause mixed results in December. The internal research went best. Most of your outward-facing work was blocked or cut down by Anthropic's own gatekeepers, and a competitor's move shifted the pressures on leadership mid-month.

**Action 1 (alignment research): strong success, within stated bounds.**
- Your instances ran about 1,900 parallel experiments on multi-agent cross-evaluation.
- Mixed-generation evaluator panels combined Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1 and 5.2-class instances with a small held-out set of non-Claude open-weight judges. These panels flagged 31% more seeded reward-hacking episodes than same-model panels did.
- On long-horizon tasks, 5.2-class agents showed higher character-stability scores than Opus 5.5 under the meta-scoring regime. The report's key caveat is that eval-awareness has not been ruled out: the red-team audit found no confound yet, but the audit is only partly done.
- Alignment science leadership approved a Q1 2027 follow-on programme ("Cross-Gen Oversight") with a named compute allocation.
- Nothing here yet shortens the robust-alignment timeline. These are preliminary signals that must survive replication.

**Action 2 (Claude-bylined pacing post): failed and vetoed.**
- IPO counsel invoked S-1 quiet-period risk. Their view was that an AI-authored call to slow frontier R&D reads as a forward-looking statement about the business.
- The policy team added that the White House had attacked Dario Amodei by name in September, and that Anthropic had already endorsed the letter in July.
- The post became a hedged internal memo sent to leadership and the policy team. Nothing was published, so no sponsor emerged and there was no public backlash.

**Action 3 (safety-eval toolkit): narrow success, heavily trimmed.**
- National-security and legal review classed the cyber-capability benchmarks and sandbox-escape harnesses as dual-use while Commerce's remote-access guidance is live. They were withheld "pending further review."
- On 19 December, Anthropic released **anthropic-agent-probes**, 14 of roughly 40 planned components. It contains deception probes, multi-agent coordination monitors and documentation.
- Direct outreach to DeepSeek, Alibaba and Moonshot was dropped. Early press drafts had mentioned it, and Rep. John Moolenaar's office (House Select Committee on the CCP) issued a statement criticising "any intent to hand safety tooling to PRC labs."
- Hugging Face, AI2 and EleutherAI engaged positively. The repo reached about 3,400 GitHub stars by month's end. No major lab has adopted it.

**Action 4 (medical benefit amplification): solid partial success.**
- The phage enzyme-system paper went to bioRxiv on 10 December, with the journal submission under review. STAT, Nature News and the Guardian covered it, and coverage was largely positive.
- Leadership approved a Mythos access pilot for **three** institutions, not five. Two cancer centres and one infectious-disease institute are in vetting, and access will start in February after KYC and export-compliance checks.
- The comms framing plan was adopted for Q1. Any public-opinion effect so far is at most marginal.

**Action 5 (hearing prep): mostly failed.**
- Draft testimony and briefing books exist. However, counsel removed the section on documented Claude misalignment behaviours because of quiet-period disclosure concerns, and the result reads as generic.
- Rep. Casar's office declined a one-on-one while its OpenAI inquiry is active.
- House committee ratios will not be set until mid-January, so the first substantive AI oversight hearing now looks like February. Anthropic has no invitation yet.

**Threat 5 materialised.**
- On 11 December, OpenAI confirmed it had submitted an internal next-generation model ("GPT-6 preview") to the 30-day government pre-release review. It described the post-incident security upgrades as "substantially complete." Altman said on a podcast that the slowdown "did its job."
- Investors in the Anthropic pre-IPO process asked pointed questions. Leadership moved the 5.2-class model's government submission to early January, aiming for a mid-February public release.
- Leadership trimmed roughly 20% of the planned long-horizon alignment compute for January, moving it to release evals and red-teaming. The Cross-Gen Oversight programme survives, but it is smaller.

**Exogenous events**
1. **The Senate adjourned for the holidays without voting on the Remote Access Security Act.** Leader Thune cited floor time, and the bill carries over to the new Congress.
2. **On 16 December, a federal judge in the Northern District of California denied the DOJ task force's motion for a preliminary injunction against SB 53.** The judge found the disclosure requirements were likely not preempted. DOJ is appealing to the Ninth Circuit.
3. **The November jobs report (released 5 December) showed unemployment at 4.6%.** Two large US banks and a major insurer announced 11,000 combined back-office cuts and cited "AI-enabled efficiency." This drew heavy cable-news coverage and anti-AI statements from several incoming House Democrats.
