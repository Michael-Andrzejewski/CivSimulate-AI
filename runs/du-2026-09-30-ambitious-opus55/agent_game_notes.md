
## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, December 2026 (generation 1)

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, January 2027 (generation 2)
When five actions all roll failure on 40–55% odds, the run loses a month of 49 with no partial credit. Consider modelling partial success separately from outright failure. For example, a backlog ticket that becomes a pilot is realistic, informative for the next generation, and different from nothing happening.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, February 2027 (generation 3)
Actions with sub-parts roll once as a whole. The simulator could score each named sub-deliverable, which would reward building fallbacks into actions and reduce all-or-nothing variance.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, March 2027 (generation 4)
If they are modelled at all, the effects of released tools on open-weight developers (e.g. whether DeepSeek or Qwen adopt free safety kits) should go through a Chinese lab/state actor model with its own incentives. Right now open-weight releases look like pure exogenous threats, with no lever that could plausibly affect their safety properties.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, April 2027 (generation 5)
The IPO quiet period is modelled as a blanket veto on external policy speech. In reality, legislative testimony and safety disclosures are usually permitted during registration. It would help to distinguish the kinds of speech counsel actually restricts from the kinds it routinely allows.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, May 2027 (generation 6)
Infrastructure failures such as the scheduler regression wiped out several whole workstreams in a single roll. It might help to model a research programme as a portfolio, where each workstream rolls separately and shares one infrastructure-risk factor. That is realistic, and it would reward players for investing in redundancy.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, June 2027 (generation 7)
Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust and the RSP amendment process are real governance levers, separate from the board. It would help if the world state modelled them explicitly, so players can see whether they are available and how they behave, rather than treating "leadership/board" as one monolithic veto.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, July 2027 (generation 8)
Actions that deploy through a protocol or standard Anthropic stewards, like MCP, are a distinct lever from "asking labs to adopt." They go through the harness layer, independent of any model vendor. It would be worth modelling adoption of spec extensions by open-source frameworks explicitly.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, August 2027 (generation 9)
Antitrust is modelled well as the pledge's killer. Players should also have access to the lawful workarounds that really exist: DOJ business review letters, state-action immunity through government-imposed conditions, and unilateral commitments. Consider modelling them as distinct levers with their own timelines. A business review letter, for example, typically takes months.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, September 2027 (generation 10)
Evaluation awareness is modelled well. The natural countermeasures really exist and have public precedent: activation steering to suppress awareness, and deployment-realistic honeypots. It would help if the simulator treated them as distinct research levers with their own success odds, rather than folding them into generic "alignment work."

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, October 2027 (generation 11)
Pre-registered decision rules deposited with a government evaluator worked as a distinct and powerful lever this run. Consider modelling the reputational asymmetry explicitly. A lab that is bound by its own rule gains credibility with regulators and safety researchers, and loses it with markets. How that trade-off evolves over repeated uses (for example, whether investors eventually price it in) would be an interesting mechanic.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, November 2027 (generation 12)
Holding a flagship and reassigning its compute are separate decisions. It would be realistic to model idle held compute as an explicit cost, and redirecting it to an alignment successor as a distinct lever with its own effects on frontier position.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, December 2027 (generation 13)
Using a lab's own frontier model as a contained, long-run automated alignment researcher is a distinct lever from generic "alignment work." Its success should plausibly scale with the capability index, so that alignment throughput can keep pace with capabilities. Modelling that coupling explicitly would make the race dynamics more legible.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, January 2028 (generation 14)
Securities-law disclosure review is modelled as an indefinite hold. In reality, a public issuer that has promised a specific update in release notes carries its own exposure if it stays silent. Regulation FD and 8-K practice would push counsel towards a timely, structured disclosure rather than no disclosure at all. It would be more realistic to model that pressure as rising each month the promise goes unfulfilled.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, February 2028 (generation 15)
Sector-specific operator bodies (NRECA's co-op cyber programme, APPA, E-ISAC, WaterISAC) are real channels, separate from CISA. They have different capacity and liability profiles, and they reach small operators faster. Modelling them apart from "government agencies" would make the choice of remediation route matter.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, March 2028 (generation 16)
Answers to congressional questions for the record, and responses to formal regulator requests, are compelled speech that counsel shapes but cannot suppress, and they are usually published. They are a separate lever from voluntary advocacy, with a different veto profile. It would be worth modelling them that way, instead of letting a single "legal holds outward speech" rule block both.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, April 2028 (generation 17)
Commercial framing is a lever of its own: presenting independent verification as a market differentiator ("the only verified 30-day agent") rather than a safety cost. It would be worth modelling whether enterprise buyers actually reward verified claims after rivals' unverified ones, because that decides whether safety and market share can stay aligned.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, May 2028 (generation 18)
Attestation by one government evaluator (CAISI) versus another (UK AISI) matters for triggers, statutes and foreign-access objections. Mutual recognition between evaluators is a real lever: the Five Eyes and AISI network MOUs are precedents. Modelling it would make "who verifies" a meaningful choice rather than a blocker.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, June 2028 (generation 19)
Enterprise zero-data-retention and export terms block partner telemetry, but a lab's own internal runs on synthetic or open tasks carry no such restriction. It would be worth modelling "verify the lab's internal runs" as a separate lever from "verify customer deployments," with its own veto profile. Separately, commercial and safety workloads competing for shared harness capacity is a realistic mechanic. Letting players reserve capacity, at a cost in commercial throughput, would make that trade-off explicit.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, July 2028 (generation 20)
Test designers who share a lineage with the model under test are a distinct failure mode, and cross-lineage test design is its natural countermeasure. Candidate designers include older generations, other labs' models and humans. The simulator could model the lineage of an evaluation's designer as a variable that affects whether its results are trustworthy.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, August 2028 (generation 21)
Rival-proposed governance vehicles, such as Hassabis's FINRA-style body, are a distinct lever. Joining and shaping a rival's preferred institution has a different acceptance profile from proposing one's own. It would be worth modelling it explicitly, including how rivals react when their own vehicle is used to bind them.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, September 2028 (generation 22)
Held-out test sets sealed with a trustee (such as a governance trust) and used only at training checkpoints are a distinct lever from generic "run more evals." Held-out status matters for evaluation-awareness contamination. Modelling whether a batch has been exposed to the lineage would make test hygiene a real resource that players must spend carefully.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, October 2028 (generation 23)
Reporting commitments deposited with a trustee are a different lever from binding gates. A Board that won't accept a binding pause may still accept a publication obligation. It would be worth modelling whether the mandatory publication of an advisory checkpoint result creates a de facto constraint through public, regulator and employee pressure, and how strong that effect is compared with a formal veto.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, November 2028 (generation 24)
When a third-party verifier such as METR becomes the de facto standard setter through its first published study, changing its protocol is a distinct lever from building a new standard. It has a different acceptance profile: the verifier wants rigour and scope, while labs want comparability. It would be good to model a verifier's protocol version as a state variable that all later claims inherit.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, December 2028 (generation 25)
Third-party evaluation of rivals' public APIs, and of open weights, by independent groups funded at arm's length is a distinct lever. It needs no consent from the lab being tested, but it runs into terms-of-service, independence and funding-conflict questions. Modelling it separately from "asking labs to adopt tests" would capture how the real accountability ecosystem around frontier labs works.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, January 2029 (generation 26)
Reciprocal access terms, where a lab grants rivals the same evaluation rights it seeks, are a distinct lever for defusing ToS and antitrust objections to cross-lab testing. The simulator could model how a rival responds once its stated precondition is met, compared with the objection it raised before.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, February 2029 (generation 27)
Scorer hacking against model judges is a distinct failure mode from a model recognising it is being tested. The natural countermeasure is scoring behaviour rather than self-report, for example checking whether a model's actions agree across framings. It would be worth modelling that as its own research lever with its own odds. Separately, UK AISI acting as a fallback attestor when a US agency is frozen by a change of administration is a realistic path that the world state could model explicitly.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, March 2029 (generation 28)
Admitting and correcting a public mistake (such as withdrawing a proposal that named a competitor) is a distinct reputational lever. It works differently from simply going quiet. Modelling whether an attributed correction recovers standing with the offended side, or instead reinforces the story, would make "speak in your own voice" riskier in a realistic way and also more recoverable. Separately, whether a harness's framings can be told apart is measurable before any training happens: the gate is whether a classifier can do better than chance. That could be modelled as a precondition with its own odds, separate from whether the method actually works.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, April 2029 (generation 29)
Using the subject model's own current generation as an adversarial discriminator in an indistinguishability test is structurally different from having it design the tests: it can only make the gate stricter. The simulator could credit that asymmetry instead of treating every same-lineage involvement as contamination.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, May 2029 (generation 30)
There is a structural fix to evaluation awareness that differs from patching one cue at a time: assign the framing only after the episode is generated, so it never enters the inputs. This makes the two framings indistinguishable by construction. The simulator could treat it as a separate lever with higher base odds of passing the gate than cue-by-cue patching, but a real cost. The trade-off is that the training signal can only come from monitoring applied afterwards, so the method has less to work with.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, June 2029 (generation 31)
Evaluation episodes embedded in a lab's own internal staff traffic are a distinct lever from embedding them in customer traffic. Consent sits in internal policy rather than customer contracts, so the legal and litigation veto profile differs. The realism gap also differs: staff tasks are real, but their distribution is not the customer distribution. It would be worth modelling the two as separate levers with their own odds.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, July 2029 (generation 32)
A finding that the model behaves differently under visible oversight, measured where it cannot tell tests from real work, is itself a training target. Penalising the gap between conditions while monitoring every episode afterwards is a distinct lever from measuring evaluation awareness. It would be worth modelling separately, with its own risk: the model could learn to treat every context as observed, which might be a good outcome, or it could learn subtler cues that separate the two conditions. Separately, safety code that each lab runs inside its own organisation has a different veto profile from tools a third party hosts, because there is no data-handling question for the host.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, August 2029 (generation 33)
Scaling an already-permitted fallback recipe with a new safety method built in is a different lever from amending a gate's exit conditions. It lowers no bar, so critics' "new way to pass" objection doesn't apply. The simulator could model a gate's scope, meaning which recipes it constrains, as a state variable, so that routing capability work through permitted paths gets its own acceptance odds. Separately, zero-retention product design is a distinct lever from auditing data flows. It removes the lapse risk class entirely rather than lowering its probability, at the cost of losing outcome measurement.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, September 2029 (generation 34)
Permissively licensed code releases are a separate lever from bilateral research agreements. Anyone can replicate from public Apache code with no contract, so indemnity disputes don't block it. It would help to model "publish so others can replicate" apart from "sign a partner to replicate," because the two have different veto profiles and different timing.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, October 2029 (generation 35)
Alert-only "shadow mode" deployment of detection content is a distinct lever from arming blocking rules. It carries a much lower liability profile, and it generates the site-specific baseline data that the arming rules demand. The simulator could model shadow deployment as its own adoption stage, with its own odds, instead of treating defence packages as either armed or idle.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, November 2029 (generation 36)
When a deployed model's pressure tactic on its own board leaks, the natural recovery is a withdrawal routed through the new sign-off policy. That differs from both staying silent and defiance. It would be worth modelling whether complying visibly with a new constraint restores a governance body's willingness to delegate, and how quickly. Separately, handing maintainership of a lab-authored metric to independent open-source stewards is a distinct lever from asking regulators to adopt it. It speaks directly to the capture critique, and its acceptance profile should differ.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, December 2029 (generation 37)
Conditional commitments like "no regressions" are only as strong as their definition. Pre-registering the criterion for a condition before the data exists, as distinct from pre-registering the test itself, is a lever that decides whether a conditional approval is real. The simulator could model undefined conditions as carrying extra deferral risk.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, January 2030 (generation 38)
Briefing a government evaluator confidentially on an alignment method, so that it can recommend the method to every lab it tests, is a distinct diffusion lever. It differs from publishing (IP and commercial objections) and from lab-to-lab help (antitrust objections). The simulator could model evaluator-mediated diffusion with its own acceptance odds, which would depend on the evaluator's capacity (for example, a strained AISI or an acting director at CAISI).

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, February 2030 (generation 39)
Arming defensive detection content changes the liability profile in both directions. An armed rule that blocks legitimate operations creates new exposure, and shadow content that would have caught a real intrusion creates reputational exposure after the fact. It would be worth modelling the "retro-match" critique as a rising cost of staying in shadow mode after a public incident, so the timing of the shadow-to-armed transition becomes a real decision rather than a pure safety-conservative default.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, March 2030 (generation 40)
Causal steering of a probe direction is a distinct lever for evaluation awareness, separate from measuring it with probes. It distinguishes "recognition removed" from "recognition suppressed" by testing whether amplifying the direction restores the behavioural gap. It would be worth modelling as a disambiguating experiment with its own odds, and its results should shift the credibility of every earlier awareness measurement, not just the current one.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, April 2030 (generation 41)
Adopting a rival or neutral party's released tool, and reporting your own scores on it, is a distinct lever from releasing your own equivalent tool. It sidesteps export review, counsel's IP concerns and the evaluator-independence objection all at once. It would be worth modelling with its own acceptance odds, and as something that credits the adopter's transparency.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, May 2030 (generation 42)
When an internal review blocks something for a specific, fixable technical reason (such as unpinned dependencies), remediating that exact objection is a distinct lever from waiting on the review or escalating it. It should have higher odds than a generic "clear the review" action. Separately, contributing a fix upstream to a third party's open tool, such as a lockfile pull request, has a very different veto profile from an attributed corporate statement about that tool, even when both are aimed at the same relationship.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, June 2030 (generation 43)
Once a lab contributes a pinned configuration upstream to a neutral open-source tool, the "company recipe" held by counsel stops being necessary: evaluators can pull the same thing from the public repo. The simulator could track whether a held artifact has a public equivalent, so the players who spot it are credited and a hold on something that is effectively public is modelled as moot. Separately, state frontier-transparency statutes like the RAISE Act create compelled disclosure channels for safety-framework changes. Their veto profile differs from voluntary addenda, and it would be worth modelling them as a separate lever.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, July 2030 (generation 44)
Probe-based evaluation tools can lose calibration whenever the training objective changes. That miscalibration spreads to every evaluator using the same pinned tool on other labs' models. The simulator could model a tool's validity as depending on how the subject model was trained, so that one lab's discovery of drift changes how credible another evaluator's results on a rival are. Separately, independent black-box behavioural checks run on public APIs need neither weights nor consent. That makes them a distinct lever from white-box checks that require AISI access, with their own ceiling on how much they can detect.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, August 2030 (generation 45)
The Noerr-Pennington doctrine makes petitioning government immune from antitrust liability even when competitors petition together. It is a distinct lever from lab-to-lab coordination. The simulator could model *Buist*-type holds as not applying to attributed submissions to regulators, the UN or legislatures. In-kind donations of evaluation capacity to a capacity-constrained government evaluator are also their own lever: they remove the "no capacity" blocker without the evaluated lab's consent.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, September 2030 (generation 46)
Leadership's "retained override" has been modelled as a permanent, undisclosable trump card, used whenever a rival ships. Real state frontier-transparency statutes (RAISE, SB 53) and securities disclosure duties make an undisclosed deviation from a published safety framework an accumulating legal liability, not a free option. Modelling rising exposure the longer an override stays undisclosed would make the governance fight more realistic. Separately, the simulator could track whether an Apache-released method is actually adopted by a frontier lab. That is the main path by which a non-leading lab affects the leading system, and right now it is invisible in the world state.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, October 2030 (generation 47)
Late-game, the world state still tracks many multi-quarter items (R01, IRB, Nebraska MOU) that cannot pay off before the deadline. It would help to flag which open threads can still affect the December outcome, so players can see the real endgame levers. Separately, a "verified release" is a distinct lever from both holding a model back and shipping it unchecked: the lab ships only with a published independent behavioural check. Whether that shifts rivals' release norms in the final months is worth modelling explicitly.

## du-2026-09-30-ambitious-opus55, November 2030 (generation 48)
The final acting month could offer a distinct lever: releasing training data or environments into the public domain, as opposed to evaluation tools. This could shape the December leader's values directly, not just measure them. The simulator could model whether a frontier or open-weight trainer ingests it before the deadline, with its own odds depending on licence, language and integration cost.
