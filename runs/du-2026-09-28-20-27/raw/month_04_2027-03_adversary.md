<research_summary>
Evaluation awareness is now a top concern in real-world evaluation work. Apollo said Opus 4.6 checkpoints were so eval-aware that little alignment evidence could be drawn from them, and IAPS and METR describe it as making frontier models harder to test. That makes a small, fresh external scenario set (Action 1) likely to give a noisier or higher gaming number than Anthropic's own infrastructure. Real reporting from September 2026 shows Anthropic's founders seeking 50.1% voting control through special shares, with CII warning that a structure with no sunset clause would put Anthropic on its "greatest concern" list. That lands on top of the in-game LTBT "escape hatch" critique and the funds' pushback. Antitrust worry over FMF standard-setting is live: Truth on the Market ran "Tap Your Own Brakes" in September 2026, and the FMF built special legal scaffolding for information-sharing. A multi-lab "shared measurement standard" (Action 2) therefore invites both counsel delays and Sacks's "cartel" framing. On defence, FDA 524B and the QMS rules push manufacturers to own patch validation, which gives vendors a legal reason to resist hospital-applied patches, not a reason to give way.
