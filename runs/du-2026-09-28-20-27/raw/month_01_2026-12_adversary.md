The biggest finding is that the world state leaves out an active fight between Anthropic and the US government. In February 2026 President Trump ordered all federal agencies to stop using Claude. The Pentagon then labelled Anthropic a "supply chain risk", the first US company to get that label. A California judge enjoined the order for civilian agencies. But on 25 September 2026 the DC Circuit upheld the Pentagon blacklist 2–1, finding that Claude's built-in restrictions could make it "unreliable" for military use. This matters because Actions 2, 3 and 4 all route through federal bodies: CISA, CAISI and congressional staff.

The Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act was extended by a stopgap only to 11 December. CISA has lost about a third of its staff, and its stakeholder-engagement division is roughly halved. That weakens the exact channel Action 3 depends on.

New York's RAISE Act will take effect on 1 January as planned. Preemption litigation is expected but will not block it quickly, so Anthropic's implementation guidance will be judged on real results.

For alt-protein, 7 or more states ban cultivated meat outright, others have moratoria (South Dakota for 5 years, Mississippi until 2030) or labelling mandates. So Action 5 walks straight into an existing farm-state political fight.
