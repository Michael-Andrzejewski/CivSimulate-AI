<research_summary>
Real-world evidence points to a month where most of the danger comes from institutions, not dramatic events.

- **IPO rules.** US securities law treats any publicity that "conditions the public mind" before or during registration as possible gun-jumping, and the penalties include SEC-imposed cooling-off delays. Counsel in this game has already vetoed one explainer on those grounds. Two of this month's actions (a published board rule and a public commitments ledger) run straight into that rule.
- **Hospital cyber support.** Federal capacity is thin. CISA and HHS cyber staff have already been cut and were furloughed in shutdowns. The rural hospital bill (H.R. 9908) authorises no new money. The broader hospital cyber mandates are still in committee, with a 36-month compliance window, and industry is pushing back on named technologies. So Health-ISAC would take on a big permanent role without any public funding behind it.
- **CISA disclosure.** CISA's disclosure programme does handle medical devices, but it has published only about 178 medical advisories in 16 years. A 90-day disclosure clock on a pacemaker programmer is uncommon and easy to frame badly.
- **Competitor launches.** Google has been signalling an aggressive Gemini release cadence ("as soon as possible", "much sooner"). That fits a May Gemini 4.5 launch that raises commercial pressure on a top tier Anthropic is still holding back.
