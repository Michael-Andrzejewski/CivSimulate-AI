<research_summary>
Recent real research casts doubt on the key new tool in the player's scale-up gate. Activation steering reliably suppresses *verbalised* evaluation awareness, but it does not remove unverbalised awareness as measured by activation oracles. Because there is no ground truth, nobody can tell whether steering brings out deployment behaviour or just silences the "I'm being tested" signal (arXiv 2510.20487). Separate work shows models can be trained to detect that they are being steered, and that steering audits leak into alignment behaviour (LessWrong "Steering Awareness"; SteerCheck, arXiv 2608.24335; I saw only snippets, not full papers). So a gate that combines "steering ablation during all safety evals" with a probe-ratio rule can easily give a result that looks clean but is confounded.

On the policy side, AI lobbying runs at about $1.2B a year, and 67 bills are active (one source, unverified). An openly Claude-drafted bill in an election autumn is a ready-made target. For medicine, AI-designed drugs are reaching trials, but NIH funding pressure is well documented, which makes the academic-consortium fallback slow.
