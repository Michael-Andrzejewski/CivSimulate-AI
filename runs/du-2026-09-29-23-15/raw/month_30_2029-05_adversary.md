<research_summary>
The strongest real-world precedent for Action 1 comes from In re OpenAI (SDNY). There, a magistrate ordered about 20M de-identified ChatGPT logs produced to plaintiffs. The court held that de-identification plus a protective order was enough privacy protection and rejected OpenAI's argument that privacy should limit production. So SDNY magistrates are comfortable with actual production, not only with review inside an enclave that Anthropic controls. Anthropic offering "raw logs" voluntarily could make things worse, because it concedes that the logs are relevant. On bio, the 2026 research literature shows over-refusal is a well-documented and politically visible failure. Some models flag 83–87% of benign biology as hazardous, and scientists say it pushes them onto open-source models. That makes a public "submit your refused prompt" form a ready-made source of embarrassing stories, and it gives adversaries a map of the filter's edges. Voice-clone fraud is growing fast: roughly $893M in reported AI-scam losses, FBI warnings about impersonation of officials, and more than 400% growth in voice-cloning fraud. Open-weight voice forks like DeepSeek V5.5 would add to this, and a single high-profile incident could pull AISI and CAISI staff away from Anthropic's patch and v1 timelines again.
