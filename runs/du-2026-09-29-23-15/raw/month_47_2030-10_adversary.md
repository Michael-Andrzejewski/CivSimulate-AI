<research_summary>
Real-world evidence points to several threats this month. First, CAISI's staff and budget are thin: its working budget is roughly $15M a year, and independent analysts call that $10–70M short of its mandate. That makes it likely that written decisions such as the Oct 29 carve-out confirmation and the 60-day re-run will slip. Second, authorized push payment (APP, or victim-coached) fraud is a fast-growing US problem, with losses projected near $15B by 2028. FedNow carries no mandatory consumer-protection or dispute mechanism, and victims who authorize a transfer are mostly unprotected under Reg E. The UK shows the problems with warning-screen defences: scammers adapt (mules with names that match the payee check), and warnings have been used to shift liability onto customers. Third, CAMARA's Call Forwarding Signal API exists and is backed by the carriers, but US production deployment is slow, which fits the world state's "quarters away" for carrier signals. Fourth, the FCC's TTY-to-RTT transition shows accessibility telecom running on multi-year, extension-prone timelines. Taken together, the likeliest failures this month are dull ones: regulator silence, deferred votes, liability framing and edge cases where one control creates a new fraud path. Dramatic incidents are less likely.
</research_summary>
