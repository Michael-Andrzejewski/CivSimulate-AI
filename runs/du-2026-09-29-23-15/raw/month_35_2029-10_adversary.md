The biggest risk this month is in the method, not in any one hurdle. Action 1 amends a pre-registration after a shortfall has already been seen. It then adds pre-freeze consumer health conversations to the sample. Research-integrity writing treats changes to a registration after the study starts as a classic source of bias ("outcome switching"). About a third of registered trials make such changes, and critics watch for them. Using the Jun–Aug health chats also runs into FTC guidance: using consumer data for a new purpose without consent can be "unfair" or "deceptive." It also runs into the existing Jun 9 court notice to preserve logs. State AI procurement is getting stricter. California's EO N-5-26 lets states exclude vendors on "responsibility" grounds, and bid protests over unequal treatment of bidders do succeed. Anthropic's "no-cost" offer and its head-to-head contest with OpenAI in Colorado could therefore turn into a procurement dispute.
