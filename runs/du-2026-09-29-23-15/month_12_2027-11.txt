The main realism problem is calendar. The simulator stages a general election on Nov 3, 2027, with a House flip, an Ohio Senate race and a "new Congress in January." Issues flagged:
- **Calendar error on the election.** Federal midterms and a House majority flip are simulated in November 2027. US congressional elections occur in even years, so a "new Congress in January 2028" is impossible. The Ohio Senate race and the Casar oversight chair also depend on this non-existent election.
- **Wrong election day.** The election is dated Nov 3, 2027, which is a Wednesday. US general elections are held on Tuesdays.
- **Overlap between Action 1 and Threat 2.** Action 1's failure and Threat 2's materialisation have the same effect (counsel blocks the OSF posting), so one roll's effect is largely absorbed by the other. Threat 2's distinct harm, the lead-plaintiff motions quoting the 10-Q, is a bit strong: lead-plaintiff motions typically argue loss size and adequacy, not merits.
- **Slightly generous protocol outcome.** The chair adds GDM's footnote on its own initiative. It is also unexplained why OpenAI staff had bandwidth to initial during their own release week.
- **Possibly fast lead-plaintiff timeline.** Appointment is set for mid-December, about four weeks after the motions. That is possible but on the quick side, and depends on the court.
